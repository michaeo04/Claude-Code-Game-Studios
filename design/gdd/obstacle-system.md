# Obstacle System

> **Status**: In Design
> **Author**: user + agents
> **Last Updated**: 2026-09-27
> **Implements Pillar**: Pillar 1 (Instant Readability) and Pillar 2 (Fair but Merciless Difficulty) — obstacles are the literal, moment-to-moment embodiment of both; see Overview and Player Fantasy.

## Overview

Obstacle System owns every hazard that protrudes from the tube's surface: where they sit, when they exist, and whether the ball has touched one. Each frame it reads Ball Movement's published state (`theta`, `s`, and their previous-frame values, plus `speed`/`omega`/`radius`) and tests the ball's swept path against every hazard currently bound to Tube Track's sliding window (a hazard's angular bounds are plain, unwrapped reals describing the counter-clockwise arc between them, so a footprint that crosses Tube Track's `+-PI` seam is one piece, not two — Formulas, F1), sending a level-triggered `hit_reported(hazard_id, run_id)` to Run State the instant an overlap is detected — and keeps reporting it, tick after tick, for as long as the overlap persists, so a contact is never lost to a pause or a freeze. It binds hazard instances to Tube Track's window slots on `segment_entered_window` and clears them on `segment_left_window` and `window_primed`, so hazards live and die with the same recycling that keeps the tube itself bounded in memory. It decides nothing about *which* hazards appear where and in what sequence over a run (Pattern & Difficulty's job once it exists), how a hazard looks or reads visually (Environment & Theming, the art bible's silhouette rules), what a near-miss or a hit *feels* like (Near-Miss Detection, Juice & Feedback), or how the ball moves (Ball Movement). It exists because obstacles are the entire test of skill this game offers: without them there is nothing to dodge, and the game has no Pillar 1 (instant readability) or Pillar 2 (fair but merciless difficulty) to deliver on — obstacles are the literal, moment-to-moment embodiment of both. The one open design tension this GDD inherits and must resolve concretely is the hidden-side "fair surprise": a hazard on the tube's far side may be unseen until the ball rotates around, by design, but game-concept.md leaves "how much hidden content is fair" and "how the player learns what killed them" as unquantified — flagged since the 2026-09-19 concept gate as this system's highest design risk.

## Player Fantasy

**The fantasy.** Every obstacle is a held breath and a release. I see it, judge the gap, commit to a line, and either taste the near-miss or I don't — this is the game's whole test of skill (game-concept.md: *"pure mastery... dodging obstacles that burst from the tube's surface a split-second before they'd flatten you"*).

**What the player feels.**
- **I always know what's coming, or I know exactly why I didn't.** An obstacle already in view is unmistakable the instant it's visible — bold silhouette, hazard-only color language (art bible). If it was hidden until the ball rotated around, that's the one exception the game allows on purpose, and even then it must still be avoidable on pure reaction, and its shape should start to feel familiar the more times I see it (Pillar 2).
- **Every gap has exactly one right answer, learnable, not lucky.** A cluster of obstacles reads as a puzzle with a solution, not a random gauntlet. Dying to a pattern once and seeing it again should feel like recognition, not déjà vu of bad luck.
- **The first look is enough — memorization is a bonus, not a requirement.** I don't need to have seen this exact obstacle before to dodge it in time; if I have seen it before, that recognition is a real edge, never a survival requirement.
- **Every death is legible in the same instant it happens.** When I hit something, I know immediately, unambiguously, which hazard it was — never "something got me" from an off-screen tangle of overlapping shapes.

**Feelings to avoid.** An obstacle that "pops" into fairness range too late to react to; a hidden-side surprise that repeats often enough to feel targeted rather than occasional; two obstacle types whose silhouettes are confusable at a glance; a hit where the player can't immediately point to the one hazard responsible.

**Serves the pillars.** *Pillar 1, Instant Readability*: obstacles are the loudest thing on screen because they're the whole test. *Pillar 2, Fair but Merciless Difficulty*: the hidden-side exception is deliberately narrow — a fair surprise once, never a systemic unfairness.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **Data model.** A hazard instance is `{hazard_id, footprint_pieces, home_segment}`. `hazard_id` is a non-negative integer, assigned sequentially per spawn within a run (never reused within the same `run_id`, matching Run State's `hazard_id` contract). `footprint_pieces` is one or more `(theta_min, theta_max, s_start, s_end)` rectangles in Tube Track's frame — a hazard type may spawn more than one piece (Double Gate spawns two), but they always share one `hazard_id`, because Pillar 1 requires the player to be able to name the *one* hazard that killed them, not which piece of it. Every piece's **raw** bounds must lie fully within `home_segment`'s `[i*L, (i+1)*L)` range (Tube Track F2), so a hazard's lifecycle ties to exactly one segment's recycling — its **effective** (ball-expanded, F1) bounds are not held to this, and may extend slightly past the raw segment boundary, which is why the overlap check between different hazards (Edge Cases, `HAZARD_OVERLAP`) also looks at neighboring segments, not just one segment in isolation. Obstacle System owns no height (`h`) dimension: every hazard reaches at least the ball's riding radius `R + D/2` (Tube Track rule 3), because the game has no vertical dodge axis — height is a rendering concern (Visual/Audio Requirements), not a collision one.
2. **Collision test (analytic, no `CollisionObject3D` — Ball Movement Rule 11, Tube Track Rule 12).** Every frame, for every hazard piece currently bound in the window, Obstacle System tests Ball Movement's published swept segment `(theta_prev, s_prev)` to `(theta, s)` (Ball Movement Rule 8) against the piece's **effective footprint**: the raw footprint expanded by the ball's own physical size — angularly by `BALL_HALF_ANGLE = asin(D / (2*(R + D/2)))` (Tube Track F6) on each side, and along `s` by `D/2` on each side. A hit is any frame where the swept segment's `s`-range and (wrap-aware) `theta`-range each intersect the effective footprint's ranges. Because this is a true swept-rectangle intersection, not a sampled point check, it cannot be tunneled through by any legally-tuned `V_MAX`/`OMEGA_MAX` pair (Ball Movement Rule 8's own callout to "the contact ADR") — no minimum-footprint-width safety margin is needed for correctness, only for readability (Formulas, `T_reveal`/`T_vis`). This per-axis (AABB) test can report a hit slightly before the ball's true continuous path would geometrically enter the footprint (a bounded corner-cut false positive, at most one frame's travel — F2) — a deliberate, deterministic bias toward never missing a real hit, not toward pixel-exact contact; see F2 and the Visual/Feel check in Edge Cases.
3. **Level-triggered reporting, for free.** Obstacle System runs this same test every frame, in every phase, with no phase-awareness of its own — Ball Movement's own published state already collapses correctly when nothing moved (`theta_prev == theta`, `s_prev == s` on a no-op or frozen frame), so the swept test degenerates to a stationary point-in-footprint check exactly when the ball is standing still inside a hazard. This satisfies Run State rule 7 (a hazard the ball still overlaps must keep being reported every tick, through a pause or a freeze) with no special-casing: Obstacle System sends one `hit_reported(hazard_id, run_id)` per overlapping hazard, every tick the test is true, in every phase, and lets Run State's own phase gate decide whether to act on it.
4. **One report per hazard per tick, not per piece.** If two or more pieces of the same hazard overlap on one tick, Obstacle System sends exactly one `hit_reported` for that `hazard_id`. If two *different* hazard instances overlap the ball on the same tick (authored hazards should never physically overlap each other — a placement-validation rule, Edge Cases), Obstacle System reports all of them; Run State's own tie-break (lowest non-negative `hazard_id` wins) is the deterministic fallback, not the primary correctness mechanism.
5. **Lifecycle follows Tube Track's window, not Run State's phase directly.** On `segment_entered_window(index)`, Obstacle System asks its content source (Rule 7) for that segment's hazards and spawns them, assigning each a fresh `hazard_id`. On `segment_left_window(index)`, every hazard bound to that segment is released. On `window_primed(first, last)` (a reset), every existing hazard is released first, then `[first, last]` is populated the same way, in increasing segment order — this is also how `run_reset` clears the previous run's hazards and re-seeds the new one deterministically (since `window_primed` fires inside `begin_run()`). Obstacle System holds no hazards while Tube Track is Idle or Uninitialized; the menu's own cosmetic hazard, if any, is Environment & Theming's and Menus & Screen Flow's concern (Tube Track Open Question 16), not this system's gameplay hit-detection.
6. **Deterministic, given the same content source and segment index.** Obstacle System itself uses no randomness; whatever supplies hazard layouts (Rule 7) is responsible for its own determinism, matching Tube Track Rule 10 and the systems-index decision that obstacles come from a hand-authored chunk library.
7. **Content source is a seam, not yet an author.** Pattern & Difficulty (which will decide *which* hazards appear where, in what sequence, at what difficulty) has no GDD yet. Obstacle System defines the seam it will fill: a `HazardContentProvider` contract, `hazards_for_segment(segment_index: int) -> Array[HazardSpec]`, called once per `segment_entered_window` and never re-queried for a segment already bound (so the provider may be stateful — e.g. consuming from a chunk sequence — without Obstacle System caring). Until Pattern & Difficulty exists, this GDD's own tests and any vertical slice use a placeholder provider (a small fixed table, Acceptance Criteria); swapping providers must not require a change to Obstacle System's core.
8. **The hidden-side exception is a budget, not a policy.** A hazard whose footprint is on the tube's far side of a fixed reference angle (`THETA_REF` = 0, the tube's own top — not the ball's live position, since this classification runs at content-population time, well before the ball is anywhere near the piece, Formulas F4) when it first becomes hittable is allowed to be first-seen late — but only if the time from when it *could* first be seen (as the camera's view sweeps toward it — an external, provisional contract from Camera, `T_reveal`, Formulas) to when the ball reaches it is at least `T_REVEAL_MIN`, so a hidden hazard is never less fair to react to than a visible one is meant to be — a related but weaker budget than Tube Track F9's `T_vis`/`T_VIS_MIN`, proxy-checked against the same floor (`T_VIS_MIN`) rather than independently derived, because unlike `T_vis` this one depends on the ball's own player-steered angle, not only on `s` (Formulas, F4). A hidden-side hazard is further capped in frequency (Tuning Knobs): never two in a row, at most one per `HIDDEN_SPAN_MIN` segments. Both checks run at content-source-population time (Rule 7), so a provider that violates either is rejected at load, the same way Tube Track rejects an unsafe `MapConfig`.
9. **No side effects beyond `hit_reported`.** Obstacle System sends no requests to Ball Movement, Tube Track or Run State other than that one event; it reads only `theta`/`s` frame values from Ball Movement, never raw world Z; and it owns no physics body or `CollisionObject3D`.

### States and Transitions

Obstacle System has no phase of its own; its behavior is a function of Tube Track's window signals and a per-frame test that needs no phase awareness.

| Situation | Event | Obstacle System does |
|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| Boot, Menu | none | No gameplay hazards bound; content source not queried |
| Map load, Idle re-prime, `begin_run()` | `window_primed(first, last)` | Release everything, populate `[first, last]` fresh (Rule 5) |
| Window advances | `segment_entered_window(index)` | Spawn that segment's hazards (Rule 7) |
| Window advances | `segment_left_window(index)` | Release that segment's hazards |
| Running, Paused, Resuming, Hit — any phase, any `dt_eff` | every tick | Runs the swept-overlap test (Rules 2-3) against every bound piece; sends `hit_reported` for every hazard currently overlapped |

### Interactions with Other Systems

| System | Direction | Data / events | Note |
|--------|-----------|----------------|------|
| Ball Movement | in | `theta`, `theta_prev`, `s`, `s_prev` (published state), once per frame | Hard dependency; Obstacle System never calls into Ball Movement |
| Tube Track | in | `segment_entered_window`, `segment_left_window`, `window_primed`, the coordinate frame (`theta`, `s`, `wrap_angle`) | Hard dependency; Obstacle System never calls `advance()` or any mutating Tube Track method |
| Run State & Restart | out | `hit_reported(hazard_id, run_id)`, every tick an overlap is true, any phase | `run_id` read from the last `run_reset` Obstacle System saw |

| Near-Miss Detection | out (provisional) | Published hazard footprints and `hazard_id`s for pieces currently in the window | Near-Miss owns what "close" means; Obstacle System only publishes geometry |

| Environment & Theming, Juice & Feedback | out (provisional) | `hazard_id`, footprint, hazard type, for visuals and hit VFX | Provisional |

## Formulas

All worked examples use the registry defaults (R = 3.0, D = 0.8, L = 12, v_max = 25, DT_MAX = 0.1, OMEGA_MAX = 3.0, BALL_LAG_TAU = 0.06, T_DODGE_180 = 1.064 s, T_VIS_MIN = 1.5 s, d_cam = 8) plus one value this GDD proposes: `GAP_MARGIN` = 2.5 (Tuning Knobs), sourced from Ball Movement F5c's own finding that "fine positioning is feasible for gaps of about 2.5 ball widths or more" — not a fresh guess. `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` is not a new tuning knob; it is the same `asin(D / (2 * (R + D/2)))` term Tube Track F6 already computes as half of its lane width `w` (F6: `w = 2 * asin(...)`, 13.5 degrees at defaults) — this section names it and reuses the value, it does not rederive the geometry. At defaults, `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` = 0.1179 rad (6.76 degrees) and `w` = 0.2358 rad (13.52 degrees). Hazard shapes used in the worked examples are illustrative placeholders, chosen to be internally consistent with F6's lane math — Pattern & Difficulty, which owns real hazard-type geometry, has no GDD yet.

**F1. Effective footprint (ball-size expansion)**

```
theta_eff_min = theta_min - BALL_HALF_ANGLE
theta_eff_max = theta_max + BALL_HALF_ANGLE
s_eff_start   = s_start - D/2
s_eff_end     = s_end + D/2
BALL_HALF_ANGLE = asin(D / (2 * (R + D/2)))                    (Tube Track F6; not rederived here)
```

A footprint's raw `(theta_min, theta_max)` is a plain pair of reals with `theta_min <= theta_max`, not pre-wrapped into `[-PI, PI)`: it represents the counter-clockwise arc swept from `theta_min` to `theta_max`, so a piece that must cross Tube Track's `+-PI` seam is authored with `theta_max > PI` or `theta_min < -PI` (for example `theta_min = 2.9, theta_max = 3.6` for a piece running from 166 degrees through the seam to -160 degrees). Obstacle System never wraps a footprint's bounds at authoring time, only when comparing against the ball's wrapped `theta` (F2).

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `theta_min`, `theta_max` | float | `theta_min <= theta_max`, `theta_max - theta_min < 2*PI` | raw (unexpanded) angular bounds of one footprint piece, plain reals, not wrapped |
| `s_start`, `s_end` | float | `s_start <= s_end`, both `>= 0` | raw (unexpanded) distance bounds of the same piece |
| `D` | float | 0.6-1.0 (Ball Movement) | ball diameter |
| `R` | float | 2.5-3.3 (Tube Track) | tube vertex radius |
| `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` | float | `(0, PI/2)` for `R, D` in their safe ranges | half the ball's angular footprint at the ball's own riding radius `R + D/2` |
| `theta_eff_min`, `theta_eff_max` | float | `theta_eff_min <= theta_eff_max` | expanded angular bounds, the ball-centre exclusion zone |
| `s_eff_start`, `s_eff_end` | float | `s_eff_start <= s_eff_end` | expanded distance bounds |

**Output range:** `theta_eff_max - theta_eff_min = (theta_max - theta_min) + 2 * BALL_HALF_ANGLE`, so an already-wide raw footprint can be pushed to or past `2*PI` by expansion; a piece whose *expanded* width reaches `2*PI` occupies the entire circumference and leaves no gap regardless of F3 (rejected at load, Edge Cases: `FOOTPRINT_EFF_TOO_WIDE`). `s_eff_end - s_eff_start = (s_end - s_start) + D`, unbounded above, `>= D`.

**Example** (a piece spanning `theta_min = -0.3, theta_max = 0.3` — a raw 0.6 rad / 34.4 degree span, about 2.5 lane-widths at `w` = 13.5 degrees — `s_start = 100, s_end = 101.5`, a raw 1.5 u span): `theta_eff_min = -0.4179, theta_eff_max = 0.4179` (effective span 0.8358 rad / 47.9 degrees); `s_eff_start = 99.6, s_eff_end = 101.9` (effective span 2.3 u).

**F2. Swept-rectangle overlap test**

```
dtheta       = delta_theta(theta, theta_prev)                       (Tube Track F1)
swept_start  = theta_prev if dtheta >= 0 else theta
swept_width  = abs(dtheta)
theta_hit    = arc_overlap(swept_start, swept_width, theta_eff_min, theta_eff_max - theta_eff_min)
s_hit        = (s_prev <= s_eff_end) and (s >= s_eff_start)
HIT          = theta_hit and s_hit

arc_overlap(a_start, a_width, b_start, b_width):
    d = fposmod(b_start - a_start, 2*PI)
    return (d <= a_width) or ((2*PI - d) <= b_width)
```

`arc_overlap` is the standard closed-arc-vs-closed-arc test on a circle, expressed with a single `fposmod` (the same idiom Tube Track already uses in F1/F8): it is exact for any pair of arc widths in `[0, 2*PI]`. `HIT` is the same per-axis (AABB) overlap Core Rule 2 specifies, made computable without ambiguity at the `+-PI` seam.

**Why this cannot be tunneled through, for any legally configured `OMEGA_MAX`/`DT_MAX`:** Ball Movement's own step never overshoots or reverses (its F1 output-range guarantee) and `s` is monotone non-decreasing within a step. So the ball's true path stays inside the box `[swept_start, swept_start + swept_width] x [s_prev, s]` at every instant, *provided* `delta_theta` recovers the ball's actual direction of travel rather than the geometrically-shorter opposite arc — which requires the true one-frame angular travel to stay under `PI`. This holds structurally (`dt_eff` is clamped to `DT_MAX` before Ball Movement sees it; the angular step is capped at `OMEGA_MAX * dt_eff`): checked against both knobs' full safe ranges together (`OMEGA_MAX` 2.75-4.0, `DT_MAX` 0.05-0.25 — not just the shipped defaults), the worst case is `4.0 * 0.25 = 1.0` rad, comfortably under `PI`. **This "no-lap" invariant (`OMEGA_MAX * DT_MAX < PI`, evaluated at both knobs' safe-range maxima together) is a precondition this formula relies on and is not yet stated as an explicit cross-system contract anywhere** (currently true by a wide margin, but should be validated wherever `BallConfig` and Run State's `DT_MAX` are both finalized — Edge Cases). Given that precondition, if the true path ever enters the effective footprint, both per-axis overlaps are true and `HIT` is true — no sampling of intermediate frames is needed.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `theta_prev, theta, s_prev, s` | float | Ball Movement's published state | the frame's swept segment |
| `dtheta` | float | `[-PI, PI)` | shortest signed one-frame angular travel |
| `swept_start` | float | any finite | start of the swept arc in the direction of true travel |
| `swept_width` | float | `[0, OMEGA_MAX * DT_MAX]` (`<= 1.0` rad at the knobs' joint safe-range maxima) | one-frame angular travel |
| `HIT` | bool | — | true iff the ball's swept path overlaps the piece's effective footprint this frame |

**Output range:** boolean. **False-positive caveat:** because this is an axis-independent (AABB) test, not a true segment-vs-rectangle test, it can report a hit slightly before the ball's actual path geometrically enters the rectangle (a diagonal path can have a bounding box that overlaps a rectangle the path itself misses). This is a deliberate, bounded, deterministic conservative bias (favor no-tunnel over zero-false-positive): a false-positive hit can occur up to one frame's travel before true visual contact. At normal frame times this is a few hundredths of a unit; at a `DT_MAX`-clamped hitch it is the full one-frame bound above. Flagged as an ADVISORY Visual/Feel check (ties to Pillar 1's "every death is legible") in Edge Cases.

**Example (catches what an endpoint-only sample would miss).** A thin "picket" piece: raw `theta_min = -0.05, theta_max = 0.05`, `s_start = 200, s_end = 200.3`. By F1: `theta_eff = [-0.1679, 0.1679]`, `s_eff = [199.6, 200.7]`. Take one frame at the config's own worst-case step (Core Rule 8: up to 0.4 rad and 3.0 u at the knobs' safe-range maxima): `theta_prev = -0.2, theta = 0.2` (`dtheta = 0.4`), `s_prev = 199.0, s = 202.0` (`ds = 3.0`). Neither `theta_prev` nor `theta` individually falls inside `theta_eff`, and neither `s_prev` nor `s` individually falls inside `s_eff` — an endpoint-only (non-swept) check would see the ball on both sides of the hazard in both dimensions and report no hit. The swept test: `swept_start = -0.2, swept_width = 0.4`; `d = fposmod(-0.1679 - (-0.2), 2*PI) = 0.0321 <= 0.4` so `theta_hit = True`; `s_prev (199.0) <= s_eff_end (200.7)` and `s (202.0) >= s_eff_start (199.6)` so `s_hit = True`; `HIT = True`. **Clean miss:** same hazard, same step size, but `s_prev = 150, s = 153.0` (nowhere near `s_eff`): `s_hit = False`, so `HIT = False` regardless of the theta overlap.

**F3. Minimum safe gap (placement validation)**

```
w        = 2 * asin(D / (2 * (R + D/2)))                (Tube Track F6, lane width)
GAP_MIN  = GAP_MARGIN * w,  GAP_MARGIN >= 2.5 (default 2.5; Ball Movement F5c)
```

At every `s0` where the set of pieces active at that distance changes (i.e. at every piece's own `s_eff_start` or `s_eff_end` — a standard sweep-line over the finitely many boundary points, no continuous scan needed between them), the pieces active at `s0` (from any hazard instance, not just one hazard's own pieces) must not, summed, leave less than `GAP_MIN` of open arc:

```
occupied(s0) = sum(theta_eff_max_i - theta_eff_min_i  for i active at s0)     (valid because Core Rule 4
                                                                                already forbids authored
                                                                                hazards from physically
                                                                                overlapping each other)
open(s0) = 2*PI - occupied(s0)
require open(s0) >= GAP_MIN  for every critical s0
```

A geometric fit of exactly `w` (bare ball-width clearance) is not enough: `w` is the width at which the ball's centre *can* physically pass, with zero margin for control precision. Ball Movement's own F5c finding — "fine positioning is feasible for gaps of about 2.5 ball widths or more" — is the sourced justification for `GAP_MARGIN`, not an invented number. A provider that violates this at any critical `s0` is rejected at load with a stable failure code (`NO_SAFE_GAP`, Edge Cases).

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `w` | float | derived, `> 0` | one ball-width of angular clearance (Tube Track F6) |
| `GAP_MARGIN` | float | `>= 2.5` (proposed default 2.5) | safety margin above bare geometric fit |
| `GAP_MIN` | float | `> 0`, `< 2*PI` for any loadable map | minimum open arc that must exist at every `s0` |
| `occupied(s0)`, `open(s0)` | float | `[0, 2*PI]` | angular arc used / free at a given distance |

**Output range:** `open(s0)` is bounded in `[0, 2*PI]`; validation requires `open(s0) >= GAP_MIN` everywhere, a hard reject otherwise.

**Example (Near-Ring, the tightest case).** At defaults, `w = 0.2358 rad` (13.52 degrees), so `GAP_MIN = 2.5 * 0.2358 = 0.5896 rad` (33.8 degrees). A Near-Ring piece's raw gap of `G_raw` becomes, after F1 expansion, an effective open arc of `G_raw - 2 * BALL_HALF_ANGLE`. Requiring `G_raw - 2*BALL_HALF_ANGLE >= GAP_MIN` gives `G_raw >= 0.8254 rad` (47.3 degrees, equivalently `7 * BALL_HALF_ANGLE`). A Near-Ring authored with a 40 degree raw gap (0.698 rad) gives an effective open arc of 26.5 degrees `< 33.8 degrees`: **rejected**. The same hazard with a 50 degree raw gap (0.873 rad) gives an effective open arc of 36.5 degrees `>= 33.8 degrees`: **accepted**.

**F4. Hidden-side reveal budget (`T_reveal`) — provisional, honestly incomplete**

**What is cleanly definable today.** A hazard is candidate-hidden when its angular distance from a **fixed reference angle**, `THETA_REF = 0` (the tube's own top, Tube Track's coordinate-frame anchor — not a runtime ball position), exceeds a fixed, purely geometric threshold: `VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH`, an external contract owned by Camera (parallel to Tube Track's `F_read`), derived from the tube radius `R`, the camera-to-ball distance `d_cam` and the camera's FOV/height (how far around the tube's curvature the camera's view — which orbits with the ball, per the concept prototype's confirmed camera model — can still see before the tube's own body occludes the far side). `hidden(hazard) := abs(delta_theta(hazard_theta_center, THETA_REF)) > VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH`, where `hazard_theta_center = (theta_min + theta_max) / 2` of the piece. **Anchoring to `THETA_REF` rather than a live ball position is deliberate, not an oversight**: Core Rule 8's checks run at content-population time, when a segment enters the window, well before the ball is anywhere near the piece — there is no meaningful runtime ball angle to compare against yet. `THETA_REF = 0` is the same worst-case-typical-position proxy already used elsewhere in this budget (the player is assumed to hold near neutral except while actively dodging, matching the antipodal worst-case framing below). This much is a clean, static, population-time-computable geometric fact once Camera supplies the real `VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH` value — exactly Tube Track's own Open Question 7, not yet resolved by any GDD.

**What is genuinely not cleanly derivable today, and why.** Even though the static classification above is now well-defined, the **live** *time* version, `T_reveal` = time from the moment a hazard's own live visibility condition (against the ball's *actual* angle this run, not `THETA_REF`) stops holding to the moment the ball reaches the hazard's `s`, depends on **when** the ball's own angle first satisfies that live condition — and `theta(t)` is exactly the one axis the player actively steers. Tube Track's `T_vis` (F9) is safe to treat as a fixed authorable budget because it depends only on `s`, an axis the player does not control. `T_reveal` has no such property: the actual reveal instant is different for every possible steering trajectory the player might take before it, including the worst case where the player holds a steady angle that never enters the visible arc until the ball's `s` is already at the hazard. There is no fixed number Obstacle System can compute at content-population time that is *the* `T_reveal` for a run that has not been played yet.

**Proposed interim treatment.** Reuse `T_VIS_MIN` itself as `T_REVEAL_MIN` (no second invented number), justified as a conservative worst-case floor rather than a real per-run guarantee: the worst realistic case at the instant of reveal is a full antipodal misalignment — exactly the case Ball Movement's `T_DODGE_180` already models, and `T_VIS_MIN` is already defined as `T_DODGE_180` + reaction + latency (Tube Track F9's own derivation). A hidden hazard checked against the same floor is, by construction, never less fair to react to than a visible one is meant to be in the worst-turn sense, even though the reveal instant itself cannot be computed without knowing the player's trajectory. Concretely: the content-source validator requires `(s_eff_start - s_ref) / v_max >= T_REVEAL_MIN` for some conservatively-chosen reference `s_ref` upstream of the piece (the same shape as F9's `NO_VALID_F` check, substituting distance-along-tube for distance-from-camera); the precise choice of `s_ref` is left as an Open Question, not resolved here.

**A second, separate provisional gap: camera lag.** Camera's own orbit lags the ball's angle (`CAMERA_FOLLOW_SPEED`, prototype value 3.0, unvalidated). Even once the geometric reveal condition above is true, the camera's on-screen view may not yet have caught up to actually showing the hazard, adding a further perceptual delay on top of `T_REVEAL_MIN`, in the same way Ball Movement's F5b latency terms stack in series. Not folded into the `T_REVEAL_MIN` proposal above; flagged as an Open Question pending Camera's own GDD.

**Why the frequency cap matters more here than the time budget can.** Because the time-based guarantee above is a proxy, not a proof, Core Rule 8's frequency cap (never two hidden-side hazards in a row, at most one per `HIDDEN_SPAN_MIN` segments) is doing real, load-bearing fairness work, not redundant belt-and-braces: it bounds how often the player is exposed to the weaker, proxy-based guarantee.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH` | float | external contract, not yet supplied | angular half-width, from `THETA_REF`, within which the tube body does not occlude a hazard; owned by Camera |
| `THETA_REF` | float | fixed constant, 0 | the tube's top (Tube Track's coordinate-frame anchor); the static reference the population-time `hidden()` classification uses, not a runtime ball position |
| `T_REVEAL_MIN` | float | proposed default = `T_VIS_MIN` (1.5 s), same safe range | interim floor for the worst-case turn a hidden-side hazard must still allow, not a proven per-run guarantee |
| `hidden(hazard)` | bool | — | true iff the hazard's angular distance from `THETA_REF` exceeds `VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH` (a static, population-time classification) |

**Output range:** not applicable — this formula is explicitly provisional; no numeric worked example is given because none would be honest without Camera's real `VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH` and an agreed player-trajectory assumption. This mirrors Tube Track's own treatment of `F_read` before Environment & Theming existed.

**F5. Hazard count budget per segment (informational, not a hard validator)**

```
S_MIN_SPACING = T_REACT * v_max
N_reads_max(L) = floor(L / S_MIN_SPACING)
```

A sanity check on density, not a placement rule: how many independent hazard "reads" (distinct silhouettes needing separate recognition — a multi-piece hazard sharing one `hazard_id`, such as Double Gate, is one read) a single segment can fairly hold, using the same simple visual reaction time Run State already uses (`T_REACT` = 0.25 s, registry `t_react`) as the minimum `s`-spacing needed to resolve one hazard before the next.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `T_REACT` | float | 0.25 s (Run State, registry) | simple visual reaction time |
| `S_MIN_SPACING` | float | derived, `> 0` | minimum `s` between independent hazard reads |
| `N_reads_max(L)` | int | derived, informational | soft ceiling on independent reads per segment |

**Output range:** informational only; not enforced at load. **Example** at defaults: `S_MIN_SPACING = 6.25 u`, `N_reads_max = floor(12 / 6.25) = 1`. At the shipped defaults a 12 u segment fairly holds about one independent hazard encounter (which may itself be a multi-piece puzzle) before density risks stacking surprises faster than the player can resolve them — a finding for Pattern & Difficulty to weigh, not a rule this GDD enforces.

## Edge Cases

**Footprint and content-source validation** (a provider that fails any of these is rejected at load — `hazards_for_segment` is never partially applied — with a stable failure code, mirroring Tube Track's map-validation style)
- **If any of a piece's raw `theta_min`/`theta_max`/`s_start`/`s_end` is NaN or infinite**: `FOOTPRINT_NOT_FINITE`.
- **If `theta_max < theta_min` or `s_end < s_start`**: `FOOTPRINT_INVALID_ORDER`.
- **If a piece's raw span is `theta_max - theta_min >= 2*PI`**: `FOOTPRINT_TOO_WIDE` — a single piece cannot legitimately claim the entire circumference; that is a map-design bug, not a valid Near-Ring (a Near-Ring is authored as a wide-but-not-full arc with a real gap, F3).
- **If a piece's *effective* (ball-expanded, F1) span reaches or exceeds `2*PI`**: `FOOTPRINT_EFF_TOO_WIDE` — distinct from the raw check above, because a raw footprint just under `2*PI` can be pushed over by expansion alone.
- **If a piece's raw `s`-range is not fully contained in its declared `home_segment`'s `[i*L, (i+1)*L)`**: `HOME_SEGMENT_MISMATCH` — a hazard's lifecycle is tied to exactly one segment (Core Rule 1); a piece straddling two segments' boundaries has no well-defined spawn/despawn moment.
- **If F3's sweep-line finds `open(s0) < GAP_MIN` at any critical `s0`**: `NO_SAFE_GAP`, reported as a structured record (the code, the offending `s0`, and the piece set active there — not message text), the same way Tube Track's `NO_VALID_F` was corrected to a structured record rather than a string a test would have to pattern-match.
- **If two different hazard instances' effective footprints intersect in both `theta` and `s`**: `HAZARD_OVERLAP` — authored hazards must never physically overlap (Core Rule 4's assumption is enforced here, not just assumed); this is checked pairwise within a segment's own new content plus its immediate neighbors, since a piece's effective footprint (F1) can extend slightly past its raw `home_segment` bounds.
- **If a hidden-side piece (F4) fails its `T_REVEAL_MIN` check or the `HIDDEN_SPAN_MIN` frequency cap**: rejected at load with `HIDDEN_UNFAIR` (frequency) or `REVEAL_BUDGET` (time), printed with the computed margin, the same reporting style as Tube Track's `VISIBILITY`.
- **If segment 0 contains any piece whose raw `s_start` is less than 11 u**: `GRACE_ZONE_VIOLATION` — Ball Movement Rule 5 requires the reset glide's span (`s` = 0 to at least 11 u at the default `STEER_ARC` = PI) to be hazard-free so the glide is never what kills the player; Obstacle System enforces the constraint the other GDD only asked for.
- **If `hazards_for_segment` is called for a segment index it was already called for** (a re-entry bug, not expected in normal window advancement): rejected with one `DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_QUERY` error; the existing binding for that segment is left untouched.

**Collision test edge cases**
- **If `dt_eff` is 0 this frame (a settling tick, Paused, Hit, or any frame nothing moved)**: `theta_prev == theta` and `s_prev == s` (Ball Movement's own no-op contract), so F2's swept test degenerates to a stationary point-in-footprint check — this is the mechanism, not a special case, behind Core Rule 3's level-triggered guarantee.
- **If the swept-rectangle test (F2) reports a hit slightly before the ball's rendered position visually appears to touch the hazard** (the bounded corner-cut false positive, at most one frame's travel): treated as a real hit — favoring never missing a tunnel case over pixel-exact contact. Flagged as an **ADVISORY** Visual/Feel check (does a death here still read as legible, Pillar 1), not a blocking correctness issue; a candidate playtest question, analogous to Tube Track's AC-27.
- **If two or more pieces of the same hazard instance overlap the ball on one tick**: exactly one `hit_reported` is sent for that `hazard_id` (Core Rule 4).
- **If two *different* hazard instances both overlap the ball on one tick** (should not be reachable given `HAZARD_OVERLAP` validation above, but the test itself does not assume it): both are reported; Run State's own tie-break (lowest non-negative `hazard_id` wins) is the deterministic fallback, never the primary correctness mechanism.
- **If Ball Movement ever publishes a non-finite `theta` or `s`** (a contract violation — Ball Movement's own guards should prevent this): Obstacle System treats it as a no-op frame (holds the last known-good swept endpoint) and logs one error; it never propagates NaN into a hit test.
- **If the ball's angular position is re-anchored mid-run** (Ball Movement Rule 5, a resume re-base): the published `theta` itself does not jump — only the internal anchor does — so the swept test sees an ordinary small step, not a discontinuity; no special handling is needed here.

**Window and lifecycle edge cases**
- **If a segment is recycled (`segment_left_window`) while a hazard bound to it is still being overlapped by the ball**: cannot happen in normal play — Tube Track only recycles a segment once it is `B*L >= C_b + M_cam` behind the camera's rear view (Tube Track rule 6), long after the ball has passed it, and recycling only happens inside `advance(s)`, which only runs in Running (never while frozen). If it happens anyway (a bug or a debug teleport, Tube Track's own Edge Cases), the hazard is released and any further `hit_reported` for it simply stops; a hit already accepted by Run State is unaffected.
- **If `run_reset` (and its `window_primed`) arrives while a `hit_reported` for the previous run is still pending acceptance**: the previous run's hazards are released immediately (Core Rule 5) and the pending report's `run_id` no longer matches the new one, so Run State's own stale-`run_id` check discards it (Run State rule 7) — the same safety net Ball Movement relies on for its own hit reports.
- **If the content source returns zero hazards for a segment**: valid; an empty segment is not an error (grace zones, pacing beats, or simply sparse content).
- **If `window_primed` fires with a range that overlaps the currently-bound range** (a re-prime, not a fresh load): every existing hazard is released first, unconditionally, before repopulating — there is no diffing between the old and new bindings (Core Rule 5).

**Cross-system invariant**
- **`OMEGA_MAX * DT_MAX < PI`, required for F2's `delta_theta` to recover the ball's true direction of travel**: validated at load using `OMEGA_MAX` and `DT_MAX` as external inputs (the same way `v_max` is an external validation input to this system's `T_REVEAL_MIN`/`GAP_MIN` checks) — `SWEEP_INVARIANT_VIOLATED` if it fails. True by a wide margin at both knobs' current safe ranges (worst case 1.0 rad against a `PI` = 3.14159 ceiling), but never previously stated as an explicit contract between Ball Movement's `BallConfig` and Run State's `DT_MAX`.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**Silhouette language for the four illustrative hazard types — guidance for Pattern & Difficulty to finalize, not a locked spec.** The art bible already fixes the grammar these types must speak: three named families (Spike, Block, Gate — §3b), told apart "by their front-on profile," each with a "tip-first identity" so the defining feature sits in the top third and reads the instant it crests the tube's horizon. Mapped onto this GDD's four illustrative types:

- **Wall** (wide arc, one gap) is a **Gate**-family piece: a flat-topped arc band with a single flat-cut gap. Its read is "mostly open, one obstruction" — the silhouette is dominated by clear sky, broken by one solid mass.
- **Spike/Needle** (narrow arc, clusters of 2-3) is the **Spike** family: tall, narrow, single-apex forms. In a cluster they should sit at slightly uneven heights or spacing (within the art bible's "proportions within 15%" per-map variation ceiling, §3g) so the cluster reads as one jagged "picket" skyline against the clear sky band (§3d), not as a single wide mass — this is what keeps a 2-3 Spike cluster grayscale-distinct from a Wall at a glance.
- **Double Gate** (two blocks, two gaps) is two **Block**-family pieces: squat, wide, flat-topped slabs. Distinguishing it from a single Wall at range is a width-and-count cue — two shorter masses with visible daylight both between them and past each outer edge — reinforced by the shared-object treatment below.
- **Near-Ring** (near-360°, one narrow gap) is the **figure-ground inverse of Wall**: instead of "mostly open, one obstruction," it reads as "mostly solid collar, one narrow escape." This inversion is deliberate and is the strongest available differentiator between the two arc-type hazards — Wall and Near-Ring should never be distinguished by degree (how wide is the mass) alone, because that degrades gracefully into ambiguity at distance; they should be distinguished by which is figure and which is ground. Whether Near-Ring stays inside the Gate family (an extreme-width variant) or is promoted to its own family is Pattern & Difficulty's call once the real roster is scoped — the art bible's family cap is 5 per map (§3b), so there is room either way.

All four must resolve within Tube Track's visible-arc window and the reaction budget this GDD's own Formulas already establish (`T_VIS_MIN` / `T_REVEAL_MIN`, F4) at the registry's default `v_max` = 25 u/s — silhouette-first design is what makes that budget survivable: a shape that needs color or fine detail to identify at range effectively shortens the reaction time the Formulas already spent effort computing. Concretely, this means chunky masses and a single dominant identifying feature per type (the apex, the flat top, the gap notch), not fine surface detail — consistent with the roughly-60-triangle-per-family budget (§3b) and the "no fillets, no bevels" abrupt-joint rule, which is itself a legibility rule as much as a style one (a beveled edge softens exactly the edge the eye uses to separate hazard from tube).

**Height is this GDD's to specify (Core Rule 1), within the art bible's floor.** The art bible sets only a minimum ("at least 1 ball diameter D," §3b, proposal). Above that floor, height differentiates the types further: Spike should read as the tallest of the four (a single apex reads as "pierce" more convincingly when it dominates the silhouette vertically); Wall and Double Gate's Block pieces should be visibly squatter and wider, trading height for arc-width as their identifying dimension; Near-Ring should be tall enough that its "near-total collar" reading is unambiguous — a short Near-Ring risks being misread as a wide Wall. These are relative relationships, not numbers; the actual values are a Pattern & Difficulty / Environment & Theming tuning pass once real geometry is authored, validated in-engine per the art bible's own caveat (§3, "numbers below... must be validated in the engine").

**Hidden-side reveal: no additional telegraph — the geometry budget carries fairness alone, and the render must not eat into it.** Player Fantasy is explicit that a hidden-side hazard "must still be avoidable on pure reaction" with no advance warning; adding a pre-reveal cue (a distant glow, an off-screen warning icon) would contradict that — it would convert an occasional fair surprise into a telegraphed one, diluting the exact narrow exception game-concept.md asked to keep, and it would do so invisibly to this GDD's own F4 budget, which assumes the reveal instant is exactly when the geometric visibility condition (`hidden(hazard)` in F4) stops holding. The only visual requirement is that a hazard render at full silhouette and color fidelity the instant it enters the camera's visible arc — no fade-in, no transparency ramp, no LOD pop-in that resolves detail progressively. F4's `T_REVEAL_MIN` is already a thin, proxy-based floor reusing `T_VIS_MIN` (Formulas, F4); any renderer-side fade or pop that delays full legibility past the geometric reveal instant silently spends part of that floor's already-thin margin without anyone having budgeted for it. This is a concrete constraint for whoever owns LOD/culling (Environment & Theming, technical-artist), not a new VFX request: hazards do not get a "materializing" moment. Color does no extra work here either — hazards are always at full chroma and peak contrast per §1's loudness budget, on-screen or not, so there is nothing to "turn on" at reveal beyond the geometry itself becoming visible.

**Double Gate reads as one object: a shared low plinth, not color or proximity alone.** Same-family material treatment (both Block pieces sharing hue, value and faceting) is necessary but not sufficient — two identical Walls placed near each other would look the same, and that is exactly the confusion Core Rule 4's single `hazard_id` is trying to spare the player from ever needing to resolve. The concrete treatment is a **shared plinth**: a continuous low-relief base band, in hazard material color, running along the full `s`-range the two pieces jointly occupy, sitting flush against the tube at a height capped well below the ball's riding radius (`R + D/2`) — comparable in scale to the tube's own seam relief (§3d, "at most 0.1D"), so it visually announces "these two pieces were built together" (a Gestalt common-region cue) without ever encroaching on the open-air gap volume the ball actually passes through. This keeps the abrupt-joint rule intact (the plinth itself has hard edges, no fillets) and keeps the actual navigable gaps reading as fully open above it — a plinth tall enough to make a gap *look* smaller than it collides would be exactly the kind of visual-collision mismatch Pillar 1 exists to prevent. This plinth is cosmetic only; it carries no footprint of its own and is not part of the two `footprint_pieces` Core Rule 1 defines. **This is a new visual element not previously named in the art bible** — proposed here, not pulled from an already-locked source; Environment & Theming should own its exact material/height once authored.

**At the moment of a hit: what this system owns, and where the boundary with Juice & Feedback sits.** Mirroring Ball Movement's own boundary-drawing, Obstacle System's entire visual responsibility at a hit is to guarantee the killer hazard's identity stays stable and queryable for as long as Juice & Feedback needs it. This is already structurally true and worth stating as an explicit contract: recycling only happens inside `advance()`, which only runs in Running, never while frozen (Edge Cases), so a hazard the ball is hit by remains bound — its footprint and `hazard_id` still resolvable — throughout the Hit phase's freeze, exactly as long as the grey-out-and-isolate beat (art bible §2, mood state 5: "grey-out isolates the killer hazard") needs it. Obstacle System does not decide which hazard is "the" killer when more than one is reported on the same tick (Core Rule 4, Edge Cases) — that tie-break belongs to Run State (lowest non-negative `hazard_id` wins) — so Juice & Feedback's isolate-and-grey-out effect should key off Run State's resolved winner, not the raw (possibly multi-entry) `hit_reported` stream this system emits. Everything else at the hit moment is out of scope here: the hitstop, the world grey-out itself, the soft white flash, and the ball's burst into cool-white shards are all Juice & Feedback's (art bible §2, mood state 5). The hazard itself gets no additional hit-state visual (no red flash, no damage decal, no material state change) — it was already at peak chroma and the darkest value on screen before the hit, and the art bible already forbids exactly this addition ("No red hit flash... breaks the reserved warm family and the loudness budget," §2).

**Audio: none owned here.** Obstacle System requests no ambient hum, no distance-based "closing in" cue, and no per-type hazard audio identity — mirroring Tube Track's own Visual/Audio section ("Audio: none... belong to Juice & Feedback"). Near-Miss Detection, not Obstacle System, owns what "close" means (Dependencies), and any proximity-based audio cue is exactly the kind of semantics that system is scoped to define once it exists; Obstacle System only publishes geometry, never a distance metric of its own.

**Applicable art bible principles.** "Danger is the loudest thing on screen... only hazards get full-chroma warm hue plus peak contrast" (§1) — binding on every hazard type at all times, reveal or not. "Shape before color... recognizable by silhouette alone" (§1, principle 2) — the basis for the silhouette-language guidance above. "Round is yours, angular is danger" (§3) and the family definitions, tip-first identity, abrupt-joint, and 1D-height-floor rules (§3b) bind the four illustrative types' geometry. The clear-sky-band rule (§3d) is load-bearing for the hidden-side reveal recommendation — it is what keeps a cresting Spike or Wall legible against a clean gradient rather than lost against background props. The numeric floors — hazard chroma at least 0.15 in hue 15-35 degrees, hazard/tube contrast at least 4:1 — bind whatever raw per-map palette Environment & Theming eventually derives (§4); this GDD does not set or vary those numbers, only depends on them holding.

**Out of scope here (owned elsewhere).** The real hazard-type roster, count, and placement sequencing is Pattern & Difficulty's (Dependencies, provisional assumptions). Raw per-map hue, prop dressing, fog and sky treatment are Environment & Theming's. The near-miss rim pulse, high-speed screen-edge streaks, hitstop, grey-out, white flash, and death burst are Juice & Feedback's (as drawn above). Camera's visible-arc contract (`VISIBLE_ARC_HALF_WIDTH`, F4) and its own follow-lag are Camera's, unauthored. This section asks nothing of any of them beyond what Formulas (F1-F5) and the Interactions table already commit to.
