## Overview

Tilt Input is the Core-layer system that turns the phone's physical tilt into the single steering signal the whole game is played with. Every frame it reads the device's gravity vector, converts it into a signed left-right tilt for the game's portrait screen, removes the player's neutral pose, filters out noise and hand tremor, and publishes one steering value plus a validity flag. It owns everything between the sensor and that value: the sensor source and its availability, the axis and sign mapping, neutral-pose capture, dead zone, smoothing and sensitivity, the effect of the app going to the background on its own state, a fallback input (arrow keys in development, a touch half-screen hold for phones without a usable sensor), and the signal that tells the game the sensor was lost. It captures the neutral where the pre-tap pose is a genuine rest pose (Menu-to-Play and `run_resumed`) and keeps it across restarts unless a steady, clearly different pose shows the phone moved. It does not own how the ball moves: Ball Movement turns the steering value into motion around the tube, and no other system reads the sensor. Because tilt is the game's only control, a wrong sign, a wrong neutral or a noisy sample is felt by the player as an unfair death (Pillar 2), and the phone must steer correctly in the postures the game supports (Pillar 4); players should feel their hand *is* the ball. Two technical decisions live outside this GDD and become ADRs, neither written yet: which loop polls the sensor (shared with the game-loop ADR that Run State also needs) and which sensor source is used (`get_gravity()` is the working choice, with the accelerometer as a fallback), both settled after the on-device spike. The published value is a normalized steering value; **the working assumption is that Ball Movement maps it to the ball's angular position** (game concept, Core Mechanic 2; technical preferences), with a rate mapping as the alternative the spike compares (Open Question 10).

## Player Fantasy

**The fantasy.** Your hand is the ball. The player holds the phone, tips it, and the ball answers right now, with no setup, no calibration screen and no sense of a sensor in between. It is the only control in the game (*Pillar 4: "Controls use a single input axis (left-right around the tube); the game is playable one-handed in any normal holding posture, seated, standing, reclined or commuting."*), so the whole "flawless reflexes" fantasy of the concept rests on it: the player must feel their reactions getting sharper, and never that the phone got in the way.

**What it feels like.** Tilting is continuous and analog: the ball's angle follows the phone's tilt, a small tip moves it a little, a big tip moves it far, and holding still holds the ball still. The game adopts the pose the player is holding when they press Play (and when they come back from a pause), and it remembers it between restarts: restarting is a tap, not a chore, and the ball starts still when the phone is held the way it was held before. If the phone was clearly moved to a different resting pose after the run stopped (not merely held steadily where it was when the run ended), the next restart adopts that pose instead. (The steadiness of the first second also rests on the hazard-free start that Pattern & Difficulty owes; see Open Question 11.)

**Where the promise holds.** Seated, standing, reclined against a backrest, or in a steady vehicle. It is **not** promised for lying flat on the back or on one's side, or for a vehicle that is braking or cornering hard: the roll formula cannot represent those postures (F1), and how far the OS-fused gravity vector resists vehicle acceleration is unmeasured (spike). A rest pose beyond `PHI_MAX - FS_eff` costs range on one side but never adds a steady bias (F2). A wrist twist at high pitch also loses gain: at `FS` 25 full lock by twist alone is unreachable above about 65 deg of pitch (F1). Section B is scoped to what the spike can confirm; a wider promise needs the F1 rework named in Open Question 6.

**Serves the pillars.** *Pillar 2, "every death traces back to the player's own choice or reaction, never to randomness or bad luck"*: sensor noise, a wrong neutral and a swapped direction must never be the cause of a death, and a lost sensor pauses the run instead of playing on with a dead control (a run never plays on a dead sensor; the short hold before the pause, at most `DROPOUT_HOLD` plus one tick, keeps the last `steer`, and V-8 measures how often it is entered). Pillar 4 as quoted above.

**When feelings conflict.** Smoothing removes hand tremor but adds lag. Fairness comes first: a steady signal beats a raw one, but any lag the filter adds must stay under what the player can feel (a budget set on a device). "Never fights me" ranks above "my hand is the ball" only when the two cannot both be met.

**Feelings to avoid.** Sluggish or rubber-band steering; a neutral that creeps or that was taken from a moment the player did not choose (right after dying); left and right swapped; a lurch in the first frames because the tap that started the run jolted the phone; an awkward posture forced on the player; a calibration chore between runs; a control that goes dead while the run keeps going.

**Reference anchors (not measured, hypotheses):** Doodle Jump (continuous tilt with no calibration step), tilt-maze games (physical feel, inertia), Temple Run (auto-run plus tilt). None was tested against this game; feel is validated on a device (spike).

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **One owner of the sensors.** Only Tilt Input reads motion sensors (`Input.get_gravity()` and its siblings). No other system does; a CI grep lint enforces it (AC-37).
2. **Output contract.** Tilt Input publishes, pulled by consumers (no per-frame signal; reading a getter twice without a poll gives the same value): `steer`, a finite float in `[-1, 1]`; `valid`, a bool; and `input_source` (`SENSOR` or `FALLBACK`). **`steer > 0` when the right edge of the phone, as the player sees it in portrait, is lowered**; Ball Movement moves `theta` in the positive direction for `steer > 0` (Tube Track: tilting right increases theta). `steer` is never NaN or infinite: a bad value becomes 0, the filter state resets to 0, and one `BAD_OUTPUT` error is logged. In Acquiring and Unavailable, `valid` is false and `steer` is 0. While a neutral capture is pending (rule 8) `steer` is 0 (never in `FALLBACK`, where no capture happens: rule 11). Consumers ignore `steer` outside Running.
3. **Orientation.** The game is **portrait, locked** by design (the camera, the HUD and the art bible assume it): `display/window/handheld/orientation` must be portrait (value 1, verified in the 4.7.2 source: landscape 0, portrait 1, reverse landscape 2, reverse portrait 3; the default 0 is landscape and does not swap the viewport size). Platform Services owns the setting and a CI project-settings lint checks it (AC-37). Godot reports gravity in **screen-relative axes in every orientation** (verified in the 4.7.2 source: iOS rotates the CoreMotion gravity into screen coordinates for portrait, upside-down and both landscapes; Android negates or swaps x and y for `ROTATION_90/180/270`), so in portrait the steering axis is the device x axis with no remap, and the sign does not flip in reverse portrait. `NOT_PORTRAIT` is therefore a **diagnostic** (the game was not built for that orientation), not a correctness guard. The tilt is how much the right edge is lower than the left, measured from gravity (F1). On a natural-landscape Android device (tablet, foldable) a portrait screen is a rotated display, and the same screen-relative output applies (UNVERIFIED on hardware).
4. **Sensor source and samples.** The working source is `Input.get_gravity()`, an OS-level virtual sensor (iOS `deviceMotion.gravity`, Android `TYPE_GRAVITY`); the sensor-source ADR settles it, with the accelerometer as a fallback where `TYPE_GRAVITY` is absent. Units are m/s^2 on both platforms (verified: iOS multiplies CoreMotion's g by 9.80665). The API has **no per-sample timestamp and no freshness flag**: it returns the last value the OS delivered. So a "sample" in this GDD is one poll-time read stamped with the poll clock; consecutive samples may repeat the same sensor value (Android delivers about 50 Hz at `SENSOR_DELAY_GAME`, iOS refreshes once per rendered frame), and every count of samples counts frames. A sample is valid if the vector is finite and `|g| >= G_MIN`; a zero vector means no such sensor or a non-mobile platform. **`SENSOR_SIGN` (+1 or -1) is per platform** and chosen by feature tag (`ios` or `android`): iOS gravity reads about (0, -g, 0) with the phone upright, Android `TYPE_GRAVITY` follows the accelerometer convention (about (0, +g, 0), from the SDK docs, UNVERIFIED locally), so the expected values are **iOS +1, Android -1**, and V-1 confirms them. F1 uses only `g.x`.
5. **Pipeline, in this order:** validity check, roll angle (F1), subtract neutral, low-pass filter (F3), then dead zone, scale by `FS_eff`, clamp to `[-1, 1]` and response curve as one remap (F4). The dead zone acts on the filtered signal, so noise does not flicker across its edge.
6. **Polling.** `poll()` takes no argument. It is called **once per rendered frame, in every phase (Menu, Running, Paused, Hit), by exactly one caller, before Ball Movement steps** (order fixed in the game-loop ADR; the recommended shape is one driver node that calls `tilt.poll()`, then `adapter.flush()` (the pause check of rule 10), then Run State's tick, then the ball step, in one `_process` callback, not relying on `process_priority`; Run State already requires `_process` for its tick, Run State Open Question 4). The driver needs `process_mode = PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS` because it polls while Paused. Polling from `_physics_process` is **not supported**: catch-up physics ticks carry wall-clock stamps microseconds apart, and the equal-stamp rule below would discard them. Time comes from the injected clock (microseconds, the same clock as Run State's `press_us`): `dt = clamp((now - previous_now) / 1e6, 0, DT_MAX)` with the shared `DT_MAX` 0.1 s. `previous_now` is the stamp of the last **accepted** poll (any poll whose stamp is greater than it, whether its sample was valid or not); it starts as "none", so the first poll of a fresh core, even one stamped 0, is accepted, has `dt = 0` and appends its sample if valid. A poll whose stamp is not greater than `previous_now` (equal or going backwards) appends no sample, uses `dt = 0` and leaves `previous_now` unchanged. `dt` is not taken from the engine's frame delta (it is scaled by `Engine.time_scale` and capped by the engine). Note: 4.5 moved physics interpolation into the scene tree and iOS can run 120 Hz frames (two `_process` calls per physics tick); the game-loop ADR decides how the ball moves relative to that, and Tilt Input only requires the poll on the render frame.
7. **Neutral capture policy.** The neutral `phi0` is the roll angle the player holds as "level". Three events matter, from the adapter (Run State's `phase_changed` supplies the previous phase of a reset; provisional):
   - **Always capture** on `run_reset` whose previous phase was Boot or Menu, or is unknown (the player pressed Play: a genuine rest pose) and on `run_resumed` (the end of the resume countdown, when the player has re-gripped the phone).
   - **`run_reset` from Hit or Paused (a restart):** capture like the events above if `neutral_stale` is true; otherwise **conditional re-anchor**. On `run_ended` and `run_paused` the core records `stop_us` (the stamp of that event) and `phi_stop`, the median of the roll samples in `[stop_us - G - W, stop_us - G]` (F2; unknown when fewer than `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` fall there). At the restart the re-anchor window is the F2 window with its start clamped to `stop_us`, so it holds only samples taken **after the run stopped** (the frozen Hit phase, or the pause; a restart within about 0.3 s of a pause has too few and inherits). It recaptures only if that window holds at least `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` samples, its spread is at most `REANCHOR_SPREAD`, `phi_stop` is known, and its median (clamped to `+-PHI_MAX`) differs from the current `phi0` **and** from `phi_stop` by more than the re-anchor offset `RA_eff = min(REANCHOR_OFFSET, REANCHOR_FS_CAP * FS_eff)` (F2); otherwise the previous `phi0` is **inherited** unchanged and the filter keeps running. A hand that has not moved since the run stopped therefore always inherits; a phone that was put down or moved to another posture recaptures.
   Reason: before a restart the pose is the death pose or the pause pose, often not a rest pose, and the window falls inside the frozen Hit phase where the player gets no feedback, so inheriting is the default; but a phone that was put down or moved to another posture leaves a steady, distant pose, which a silent permanent bias would punish (Pillar 2). **A steady death tilt inherits:** under a position mapping a steady large tilt is normal play, so a recapture must also differ from `phi_stop` (Open Question 22 now only measures the residual misfires). The neutral is otherwise **fixed** (no continuous recentering, no manual recalibration in the MVP). `neutral_stale` is true at construction, set when the app is backgrounded (rule 9) or a sensor is lost (rule 10), and cleared by any capture.
8. **Capture mechanics.** Tilt Input keeps a ring buffer of roll angles stamped with the injected clock: capacity 256, a fixed `BUFFER_AGE` of 1.0 s, oldest overwritten, samples older than `BUFFER_AGE` dropped at every append and at every capture. **All durations in seconds (`G`, `W`, `DROPOUT_HOLD`, `SENSOR_RESUME_SETTLE`, `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT`, `BUFFER_AGE`) are converted once at load with `roundi(x * 1e6)` and every comparison runs on integer microseconds**, so a closed window cannot be off by one from float rounding. A capture sets `phi0` to the **median of the samples in `[t - G - W, t - G]`** (F2), where `t` is the handler's time; the guard skips the jolt of the tap that caused the capture. If fewer than `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` fall in the window, or Tilt Input is not Live, `neutral_pending` is set (a conditional re-anchor with too few samples does **not** set pending: it inherits). A new capture event while `neutral_pending` is already true **restarts the accumulator** (the samples gathered so far are discarded and counting starts from zero). While `input_source` is `FALLBACK` every capture event is a no-op (rule 11). A **pending capture takes the median of the first `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` valid samples after the event** (so an outlier or a one-frame jolt does not decide it) and `steer` is 0 until then. `phi0` is clamped to `+-PHI_MAX` (F2). After a capture the filter starts at 0 (the relative angle is about 0 by construction, and the jolt of the tap is filtered instead of going straight into `steer`). On entering Live from Acquiring or Unavailable without a capture, the filter starts at the current relative angle. The settling tick (Run State rule 14) only gives the filter extra polls; nothing depends on it.
9. **Availability and the app lifecycle.** At boot, on **Android**, the node reads `ProjectSettings` (`input_devices/sensors/enable_gravity`, `enable_accelerometer`; both default false and require a restart) and passes `sensors_enabled` to the core; if the chosen sensor is disabled it logs `SENSORS_DISABLED`; if the sensor-source ADR keeps the accelerometer as a fallback for a device without `TYPE_GRAVITY`, both `enable_gravity` and `enable_accelerometer` must be true (each registers a listener), and `get_setting` is called with an explicit default. On **iOS** the CoreMotion manager starts unconditionally (verified: `godot_commonInit`), so `sensors_enabled` is true there. **Platform Services owns the OS lifecycle notifications** and exposes two signals, provisionally `app_backgrounded` and `app_foregrounded`, which it derives itself (its GDD F1: on Android from `FOCUS_OUT/IN`, because `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_PAUSED/_RESUMED` come only from the GL renderer and may never fire under Vulkan; on iOS from `PAUSED/RESUMED`; Open Question 24); Tilt Input's adapter connects them to `on_app_backgrounded()` and `on_app_foregrounded()` on the core, so one node owns one notification and there is no tree-order dependency. On backgrounded: the buffer is cleared, `neutral_stale` is set and the state returns to Acquiring; in Acquiring the buffer is still cleared and the settle restarted, with no state change and no signal. On foregrounded: samples are discarded for `SENSOR_RESUME_SETTLE`, because Android unregisters the listener when the app pauses (verified) while Godot keeps the last vector, which would pass the validity check with fresh poll stamps. On iOS `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_PAUSED` fires only when the app enters the background; the control centre, the notification shade and the app switcher fire only focus-out, which stops rendering while CoreMotion keeps running and is re-read live, so no stale vector arises there (verified). Delivery of the notifications to a plain Node is verified in the 4.7.2 source (`SceneTree` propagates them to every node; on iOS `PAUSED` fires when the app enters the background; on Android `FOCUS_OUT` comes from `onPause`, the same point where the sensors are unregistered, while `PAUSED/_RESUMED` come only from the GL renderer); Platform Services confirms the sequences on a device (its PS-1 to PS-3).
10. **Sensor loss and the pause.** While Live with `SENSOR`, invalid polls are counted by F6: `steer` holds its last value while the accumulated clamped poll time of consecutive invalid polls is at most `DROPOUT_HOLD` **or** fewer than `DROPOUT_MIN_POLLS` (3) polls have been invalid (`valid` stays true, so a one-frame blip or a frame hitch is invisible). After that the state is Unavailable: `valid` false, `steer` 0, `neutral_stale` set, `availability_changed(false)` emitted. A valid sample brings the state back to Live (`availability_changed(true)`) and the next capture event takes a fresh neutral. **The pause is level-triggered and evaluated by the driver, outside any Run State handler.** Run State rejects, with an Error, a request sent from inside one of its event handlers or its tick (its rule 3: transitions never nest), so the adapter never sends the request from `run_started`, `phase_changed` or `availability_changed`. Instead the adapter (`TiltRunAdapter`, rule 12) tracks the phase through its injected `phase_source` and exposes `flush()`. The driver calls `flush()` **once per frame, after `tilt.poll()` and before Run State's tick** (rule 6); `flush()` requests `pause_requested(sensor_lost)` iff `sensor_lost_pause_needed(phase, valid)` (a pure function, AC-43): `valid` is false and the phase is Running or Resuming. The request is queued and processed by the tick that follows, so a run that would start with `valid` false (a race, see Edge Cases) is paused on its settling tick (Run State accepts a pause there), and a run is never played on with a dead control. At most one request is sent per frame and a repeated request is a no-op in Run State. **Restart is gated like Play and Resume:** the HUD and Menus disable Play, Resume and Restart and swallow the tap-anywhere restart in Hit while `valid` is false, with a "sensor not ready" cue, so a dead sensor never burns a run (UI Requirements). The message distinguishes `state` Acquiring ("reconnecting...", it heals by itself) from Unavailable ("no motion sensor"). The pause screen must always offer a way to the Menu, so the state is never a dead end.
11. **Fallback input.** `input_source` becomes `FALLBACK` (a) at construction in a debug build with sensors disabled, and (b) when Acquiring times out (no valid sample within `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT`, counted from the first poll after the settle), **in any build, but only if no valid sample was ever accepted this session** (`sensor_ever_live` is false). If a sensor was ever live, the same timeout leaves Acquiring for Unavailable instead (one `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` error), the control scheme never changes mid-session, and a later valid sample returns to Live. Entering FALLBACK clears `neutral_pending` and `neutral_stale`, and capture events are no-ops there (there is no neutral to capture). In FALLBACK the state is Live, `valid` is true, and `steer` follows a target: `target = clamp(key + touch, -1, 1)`, `steer = move_toward(steer, target, FALLBACK_SLEW * dt)` (F6). `key` comes from `steer_left` / `steer_right` (debug builds only, guarded by `InputMap.has_action()`; the actions must exist in `project.godot`), and `touch` from a **touch half-screen hold forwarded by the HUD / Menus** (left half -1, right half +1, both 0), sent only while the phase is Running and released on leaving Running so it never clashes with tap-anywhere restart; a touch counts only if its press-down came after `run_started` (the thumb that restarted the run does not steer) and the HUD pause button's area is excluded. Tilt Input itself reads no touch (rule 13); the core takes a `fallback_source: Callable() -> int` in `{-1, 0, +1}`. FALLBACK is terminal for the session (it is a fallback for phones without a usable sensor and a development aid, not a selectable control scheme), and while `input_source` is `SENSOR` the fallback is ignored. `sensors_enabled = false` in a **release** build is a configuration error, not a device limitation: the state is Unavailable with one `SENSORS_DISABLED` error and it stays Unavailable through app backgrounding and foregrounding (the lint in AC-37 keeps it from shipping). Mouse control is post-MVP (game concept). `input_source` is read at `run_started`, so Scoring & Personal Best can flag a run played with `FALLBACK` as a different control scheme (Open Question 27).
12. **Testing seam.** `TiltMath` holds the pure functions (F1-F4, `should_reanchor` and `sensor_lost_pause_needed`) with no validation. `TiltCore` is a `RefCounted` with no Node and no `Input` call: it takes an injected `sample_source: Callable() -> Vector3`, a `clock: Callable() -> int`, a `log_sink: Callable(level, code, detail)`, a `fallback_source: Callable() -> int`, `is_debug`, `sensors_enabled`, `is_portrait` and a `TiltConfig` (which carries the resolved `sensor_sign`). Events in: `poll()`, `on_run_reset(previous_phase)`, `on_run_resumed()`, `on_run_stopped()` (from `run_ended` and `run_paused`: records `stop_us` and `phi_stop`, rule 7), `on_app_backgrounded()`, `on_app_foregrounded()`. `TiltConfig.validated()` applies rule 14; a test-only `TiltConfig.unvalidated()` bypasses it and must not be referenced from `src/` (AC-37). `PHI_MAX`, `FS_EFF_MAX`, `F_MIN`, `BUFFER_AGE`, `TAU_FLOOR`, `DROPOUT_MIN_POLLS` and `REANCHOR_FS_CAP` are constants of `TiltMath` / `TiltCore`, not config fields. Log codes (structured): `SENSORS_DISABLED`, `SENSOR_TIMEOUT`, `NOT_PORTRAIT`, `KNOB_CLAMPED`, `BAD_OUTPUT`, `POSTURE_UNSUPPORTED`. The core logs every rejected input; the production sink rate-limits to one message per code per 1.0 s of clock. Read-only getters: `steer`, `valid`, `input_source`, `state`, `phi0`, `phi_f`, `phi_stop`, `neutral_pending`, `neutral_stale`, `sensor_ever_live`, `sample_count`. A thin `TiltInput` node reads the sensor, `ProjectSettings` and the fallback keys and forwards them; the Run State glue is a separate `TiltRunAdapter` (`RefCounted`, no Node) with an injected `request_pause: Callable(source)` and `phase_source: Callable() -> phase`, whose cache of the last `phase_changed` is initialized from `phase_source` at construction (so the first Menu-to-Play reset has a known previous phase) and which exposes `flush()` (rule 10); it can be built in a test with Run State's SceneTree-free core (its AC-8), so its ACs are not deferred (AC-39); a constructor given an invalid Callable logs one error and leaves the core Unavailable (guard with `is_valid()` at init). GDScript pitfalls for the seam: Callables are untyped, lambdas capture locals by value (tests mutate a member or a one-element Array), a lambda capturing `self` and stored inside a `RefCounted` creates a leaking cycle (use bound methods or a `dispose()`), `class_name` collisions can occur in headless runs (`preload` the two classes to avoid the class cache), and the ring buffer should be a `PackedFloat64Array` of angles plus a `PackedInt64Array` of stamps with a head index and no getter exposing them (getters return copies). Tests build a fresh `TiltCore` each.
13. **No touch inside Tilt Input.** Tilt Input never reads touch or gestures (Pillar 4); touch belongs to HUD and Menus, which forward the fallback hold through `fallback_source` (rule 11).
14. **Config validation.** At load, in this order: (1) each knob is clamped to its own safe range (a non-finite knob takes its default); `sensor_sign` other than +1 or -1 takes the platform's expected value; (2) if `G + W > 0.9` (compared in integer microseconds), `W` is reduced to `0.9 - G`; (3) `SENSOR_RESUME_SETTLE` is clamped to at most `1.0 - G - W`; (4) `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` is clamped to at most `floor(W * F_MIN)` (computed in integer microseconds, so 0.15 s at 20 Hz gives exactly 3); (5) `DEAD_ZONE` is clamped to at most `0.15 * TILT_FULL_SCALE`. One `KNOB_CLAMPED` error per knob changed (`CURVE_EXP < 1` becomes 1; a `FILTER_TAU` of 0 or less becomes 0.02). The bound `SETTLE + G + W <= 1.0` comes from the shortest resume countdown (1.0 s, Run State): after an app pause the buffer is empty, samples are accepted only after `SETTLE`, and the window at `run_resumed` must fit after that.

### States and Transitions

| State | Meaning | `valid` | `steer` |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| Acquiring | Sensors enabled, waiting for the first valid sample (at boot, or after the app was backgrounded) | false | 0 |
| Live | Valid samples flowing (`input_source` = `SENSOR`), or the fallback input (`input_source` = `FALLBACK`) | true | pipeline output, or fallback-driven |
| Unavailable | No usable sensor: lost mid-session, or sensors disabled in a release build (a configuration error) | false | 0 |

|------|-------|----|
| (construct) | sensors enabled | Acquiring |
| (construct) | sensors disabled, debug build | Live (`FALLBACK`) |
| (construct) | sensors disabled, release build | Unavailable (configuration error) |
| Acquiring | first valid sample (after any resume settle) | Live (`SENSOR`) |
| Acquiring | no valid sample within `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT` (counted from the first poll after the settle), any build, `sensor_ever_live` false | Live (`FALLBACK`) |
| Acquiring | the same timeout, `sensor_ever_live` true | Unavailable (one `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` error) |
| Live (`SENSOR`) | invalid polls past the hold (F6) | Unavailable |
| Live (`SENSOR`), Unavailable (sensor lost) | app backgrounded (rule 9) | Acquiring |
| Unavailable | valid sample | Live (`SENSOR`) |

`sensor_ever_live` is set by the first valid sample accepted in `SENSOR` mode and is never cleared. Unavailable from a release-build configuration error ignores every event and never leaves. Every other pair (including Live plus a valid sample, anything from Live with `FALLBACK`, and Acquiring plus app backgrounded, which only clears the buffer and restarts the settle) is ignored: no state change, no error, no signal. `availability_changed(available: bool)` is emitted **whenever `valid` changes value**; the value at construction is not a change (so booting into `FALLBACK` or Unavailable emits nothing, and Acquiring to Live emits `true`, including Acquiring to Live `FALLBACK`). At construction `neutral_pending` and `neutral_stale` are true (entering `FALLBACK` clears both): the first `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` valid samples capture the neutral (their median) and start the filter at 0, and `steer` is 0 until then.

**Run lifecycle (events, not states):** `run_reset` and `run_resumed` are capture events under rule 7; `run_paused` and `run_ended` only record `stop_us` and `phi_stop` (rule 7) and `run_started` changes nothing in the core; the level-triggered pause is evaluated by the adapter's `flush()` every frame, not by these events (rule 10).

## Formulas

Tilt angles are in **degrees** (`asin` returns radians: convert when implementing). Every default value is in Tuning Knobs and is a guess until the on-device spike. Numbers were re-computed independently (Node) on 2026-09-21; nothing was run in Godot. Oracle values in the Acceptance Criteria use `dt = 0.016667` (a 16667 us tick), not `1/60`.

**Variables (shared)**

| Variable | Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|----------|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| Gravity | g | Vector3 (m/s^2) | finite, `\|g\| >= G_MIN` | from `Input.get_gravity()`, screen-relative device axes; the raw sign convention is per platform, unverified |
| Roll | phi | float (deg) | [-90, 90] | left-right tilt; the right edge lower than the left is positive |
| Neutral | phi0 | float (deg) | +-`PHI_MAX` (70) | the captured pose |
| Relative, filtered | phi_r = phi - phi0, phi_f | float (deg) | +-(90 + 70) | before and after the filter |
| Step | dt | float (s) | [0, `DT_MAX`] | clamped clock difference (`DT_MAX`, the shared 0.1 s) |
| Filter gain | alpha | float | [0, 1) | filter coefficient |
| Effective full scale | FS_eff | float (deg) | 6-50 | see F4 |
| Steer | s | float | [-1, 1] | the published value |

**F1. Roll angle**

`phi = SENSOR_SIGN * deg(asin(clamp(g.x / |g|, -1, 1)))`

- Output [-90, 90]. A zero, NaN, infinite or below-`G_MIN` vector is rejected **before** F1. The clamp is needed because rounding can push `g.x / |g|` slightly above 1 (it lives in a helper `clamp_ratio` so it can be tested, AC-1).
- Example (pitch 30 deg, twist 30 deg): `g = (4.248, -7.358, -4.905)` gives `phi = asin(0.433) = 25.66` deg (iOS convention, `SENSOR_SIGN` +1).
- Noise gain is `1 / cos(phi)`: a 0.1 m/s^2 error is 0.58 deg at `phi` 0 but about 3.3 deg at `phi` 80, above the dead zone. Beyond about 70 deg the signal is unusable, and `asin` folds at 90 (tilting past it reads the same as tilting back): postures with `|phi|` above `PHI_MAX` are unsupported, which excludes lying on one's side.
- This is the geometric "right edge lower than the left" angle. A wrist twist about the screen normal loses gain as the phone is pitched back: a 50 deg twist reads 41.6, 22.5 and 3.8 deg at pitch 30, 60 and 85 (gravity cannot see the yaw part of a twist). Reaching `phi = FS_eff` by twist alone needs `sin(twist) = sin(FS_eff) / cos(pitch) <= 1`: at `FS_eff` 25 that is impossible above pitch 65 deg, and at pitch 60 it needs a 57.7 deg twist. Two fixes are deferred (Open Question 6): rotating `g` by `-phi0` about the screen normal before `asin`, and a blend with `atan2(g.x, -g.y)`.
- Vehicle acceleration adds an error of at most `asin(a / |g|)` to a raw accelerometer (11.8 deg at 2 m/s^2). `get_gravity()` is OS-fused, so the real figure is unknown, and fusion adds latency; the spike measures both.

**F2. Neutral capture**

`m = median{phi_i : t - G - W <= t_i <= t - G}` (closed interval; for an even count, the mean of the two middle values), `N` = the count, `spread = max - min` over the same samples. (`G` = `NEUTRAL_GUARD`, `W` = `NEUTRAL_WINDOW`, `N_min` = `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES`, `RA_O` = `REANCHOR_OFFSET`, `RA_S` = `REANCHOR_SPREAD`.)

- **Capture** (Menu or Boot reset, `run_resumed`, or a reset while `neutral_stale`): needs `N >= N_min`, otherwise `neutral_pending` (rule 8, the median of the first `N_min` valid samples afterwards).
- **Conditional re-anchor** (`run_reset` from Hit or Paused, not stale): the window is `[max(t - G - W, stop_us), t - G]` (samples taken after the run stopped); `m_c = clamp(m, -PHI_MAX, +PHI_MAX)`; `phi_stop` is the median of the samples in `[stop_us - G - W, stop_us - G]` (recorded at `run_ended` or `run_paused`); `RA_eff = min(RA_O, REANCHOR_FS_CAP * FS_eff)`. Recapture iff `N >= N_min` and `spread <= RA_S` and `phi_stop` is known and `|m_c - phi0| > RA_eff` and `|m_c - phi_stop| > RA_eff`; otherwise inherit. Comparing the clamped median keeps a rest pose beyond `PHI_MAX` from recapturing at every restart. `REANCHOR_FS_CAP` (0.5) keeps an inherited neutral from leaving a saturating bias at high sensitivity (`RA_eff` is 6.25 deg at sensitivity 2); at the defaults `RA_eff` is 12. The largest steady bias an inherited neutral can leave is `(RA_eff - dz_eff) / (FS_eff - dz_eff)` of full lock: 0.447 at the defaults. The decision is the pure function `should_reanchor` (AC-16g).
- `phi0 = clamp(m, -PHI_MAX, +PHI_MAX)`. With `L_eff = max(0, PHI_MAX - FS_eff)`, when `|m| > L_eff` one `POSTURE_UNSUPPORTED` error is logged.
- Example: 18 samples in the window with median 3.4 gives `phi0 = 3.4`. The window is a closed interval: a 0.3 s window holds 9 or 10, 17 or 18 and 30 or 31 samples at 30, 60 (16667 us) and 100 Hz; a dropped frame lowers the count, and at 20 Hz it can be as low as 4 to 5 (Open Question 28); every sample is a frame read (rule 4).
- **A rest pose beyond `L_eff` loses range, it does not add bias.** Rest at 60 deg with `FS_eff` 25 (`L_eff` 45): `phi0 = 60`, `steer` is 0 at rest, full lock toward the near side is intact, and toward the far side the roll can reach 70 deg, so `steer` reaches at most `(10 - 1.5) / 23.5 = 0.362`. That is the signal that the posture is unsupported.
- Constraints: `N_min <= floor(W * F_MIN)`, `G + W <= 0.9` and `SETTLE + G + W <= 1.0` (rule 14); the window fits inside the shortest resume countdown and the buffer.

**F3. Low-pass filter**

`alpha = 1 - exp(-dt / max(tau, TAU_FLOOR))`, `phi_f = phi_f + alpha * (phi_r - phi_f)`, `TAU_FLOOR = 0.005`.

- Start: `phi_f = 0` after every capture; `phi_f = phi_r` on entering Live from Acquiring or Unavailable without a capture; unchanged on an inherited neutral. A loaded config never has `tau < 0.02`; the `TAU_FLOOR` guard is defense in depth for direct `TiltMath` calls. Invalid polls do not update the filter; a valid sample uses the `dt` of its own poll (the stamp difference to the previous accepted poll, clamped).
- Example: `alpha = 0.2835` at dt = 0.016667 with tau = 0.05; 0.8647 at dt = 0.1. Gain 0.954, 0.847 and 0.303 at 1, 2 and 10 Hz; phase lag 48, 45 and 20 ms (continuous-time approximations; the exact oracles are AC-5 and AC-9).
- Extremes: dt = 0 gives alpha = 0 (holds); a NaN in `phi_f` is reset to 0 by the `BAD_OUTPUT` rule.
- **Noise rejection depends on the frame rate.** For an alternating +-A input the steady state passes `A * alpha / (2 - alpha)`: 0.33 A at 30 Hz, 0.165 A at 60 Hz, 0.083 A at 120 Hz (with tau fixed). AC-9 is therefore a 60 Hz claim: at 30 Hz the same +-4 deg input reaches `|phi_f|` 1.946 on the first tick, above the dead zone. V-2 measures the noise at the device's real frame rate; scaling tau with the frame rate is Open Question 25.

**F4. Dead zone, scale, clamp, response curve**

`FS_eff = min(FS / sensitivity, FS_EFF_MAX)`; `dz_eff = min(DZ, 0.2 * FS_eff)`;
`u = clamp(sign(phi_f) * max(0, |phi_f| - dz_eff) / (FS_eff - dz_eff), -1, 1)`; `s = sign(u) * |u|^k`.
(`FS` = `TILT_FULL_SCALE`, `DZ` = `DEAD_ZONE`, `k` = `CURVE_EXP`, `FS_EFF_MAX` = 50.)

- Output [-1, 1], continuous at the dead-zone edge (no jump, no hysteresis needed) and monotone for `k >= 1`. The denominator is at least `0.8 * FS_eff`, so at least 4.8.
- Example: `FS = 25`, `DZ = 1.5`, `phi_f = 14`, `k = 1.5` gives `u = 0.532` and `s = 0.388`.
- Extremes: `FS = 12` with `sensitivity = 2` gives `FS_eff = 6` and the dead zone caps at 1.2 (the dead zone shrinks as sensitivity rises while tremor noise in degrees does not, and the players who need high sensitivity are also the more tremor-prone: V-2 is re-checked at sensitivity 2). `FS = 45` with `sensitivity = 0.5` would need 90 deg for full lock; `FS_EFF_MAX` holds it at 50, and with `FS` 45 every sensitivity from 0.5 to 0.9 gives the same `FS_eff` of 50 (the slider range in Settings is defined against the shipped `FS`). `k >= 1` is required (`k < 1` amplifies noise).

**F5. Latency budget (design-time check, not runtime logic)**

`L_tilt ~ L_s + tau + T / 2` with `T` the frame time. A discrete filter with a zero-order hold lags a ramp by about `tau - T / 2`, so the sum is conservative by roughly half a frame; a sensor slower than the frame rate adds about half its period. `L_s` (sensor latency, including any OS fusion) is unmeasured: 30 ms is a placeholder and may be larger. The default `tau = 0.05` is checked against a target chosen after the spike (P-1); nothing in the running game evaluates this.

**F6. Sensor loss and the fallback input**

Consecutive invalid polls accumulate `invalid_us += min(dt_us, DT_MAX_us)` (integer microseconds, `dt_us` the stamp difference to the previous accepted poll) and `invalid_polls += 1` (a poll rejected by the stamp rule of rule 6 counts neither); a valid sample resets both. While `invalid_us <= HOLD_us` **or** `invalid_polls < DROPOUT_MIN_POLLS`: `steer` holds its last published value, `valid` stays true, the state stays Live. Otherwise the state is Unavailable, `valid` false, `steer` 0. Because each poll adds at most `DT_MAX`, a frame hitch of any length counts as at most `DT_MAX` and one poll, so a hitch plus one invalid sample never pauses the run (Run State's stall guard tolerates hitches up to 0.5 s). `HOLD = 0` is allowed: the third consecutive invalid poll then makes the state Unavailable, not the first.

- Example at 50 Hz (`dt_us` 20000, `HOLD` 0.1 s): the 5th invalid poll has `invalid_us` 100000 (not greater than 100000, still held), the 6th 120000 (Unavailable).
- Fallback: `s = move_toward(s, target, FALLBACK_SLEW * dt)` with `target` from rule 11; both keys or both halves give a target of 0. At `FALLBACK_SLEW = 4` per second, 0 to 1 takes 0.25 s.

## Edge Cases

**Sensor and samples**
- **If the chosen sensor's project setting is false on Android**: log one `SENSORS_DISABLED` error at boot naming the settings; the state is Live with `FALLBACK` in a debug build and Unavailable in a release build (a configuration error that must not ship). Tilt Input never silently steers 0 without that log line. On iOS the setting is not read.
- **If the vector is zero, NaN or infinite**: the sample is invalid; nothing reaches F1. A finite but huge vector may overflow `|g|` and give `phi` 0 (wrong but finite, no error; an upper bound is Open Question 7).
- **If `|g| < G_MIN` (free-fall, a thrown or dropped phone)**: the sample is invalid; rule 10 applies (hold, then Unavailable and a pause). The fused `get_gravity()` keeps a near-constant magnitude, so `G_MIN` is mostly inert there; on the raw accelerometer fallback a walking or bus jolt can trip it (V-8 measures spurious pauses).
- **If the platform reports gravity in g units** (`|g|` near 1): every sample is invalid (`G_MIN` 3), so the state times out to `FALLBACK` with one `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` error; both platforms deliver m/s^2, so the spike (V-5) confirms this once.
- **If the phone is accelerating (a braking bus, cornering, shaking)**: F1 uses the direction only; the error is unmeasured for `get_gravity()` (F1). Nothing detects it in the MVP.
- **If the sensor returns the same vector for a long time (stuck)**: not detectable by comparing vectors (the API repeats the last value by design); Open Question 7.
- **If the chosen source returns zero but another sensor returns valid data (no `TYPE_GRAVITY`, accelerometer present)**: the fallback order is decided in the sensor-source ADR; until then the state times out to `FALLBACK`.
- **If the screen is not in portrait at boot (the lock setting is missing)**: log one `NOT_PORTRAIT` error as a diagnostic; F1 still uses the screen-relative x axis, and the layout (not the sign) is what is wrong.
- **If there is no usable motion sensor at all (a phone without one, an emulator)**: if no valid sample was ever accepted this session, after `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT` the state is Live with `FALLBACK` in any build, with one `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` error; the touch half-screen hold steers, and the HUD shows the "no motion sensor" notice once.
- **If a sensor that worked earlier this session does not return after the app was backgrounded (no valid sample within `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT` after the settle)**: the state leaves Acquiring for Unavailable with one `SENSOR_TIMEOUT` error, `input_source` stays `SENSOR`, the control scheme never changes mid-session, Play, Resume and Restart stay disabled and the pause screen offers the Menu; a later valid sample returns to Live (AC-49).

**Neutral pose**
- **If the player restarts from Hit or Paused and the pose is unchanged or unsteady**: the previous `phi0` is inherited and the filter keeps running (a spread above `REANCHOR_SPREAD`, a median within `RA_eff` of `phi0` or of `phi_stop`, an unknown `phi_stop`, or too few samples taken after the run stopped, means inherit).
- **If the player restarts from Hit or Paused and the phone was put down or moved to a steady pose more than `RA_eff` from both `phi0` and `phi_stop`**: the window's median (clamped to `+-PHI_MAX`) becomes the new `phi0`, the filter starts at 0 and no signal is emitted. **A steady death tilt inherits:** a player who dies holding a large steady tilt and does not move the phone has a median equal to `phi_stop`, so the neutral is kept (AC-16h). Residual risk: a player who moves the phone by more than `RA_eff` right after death and holds it steady is treated as a posture change (Open Question 22 measures how often).
- **If the window holds fewer than `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` at a restart that is not stale (a long hitch)**: the previous `phi0` is inherited; the re-anchor never sets pending.
- **If the rest pose is beyond `L_eff`**: `phi0` is passed through up to `+-PHI_MAX` with one `POSTURE_UNSUPPORTED` error; `steer` is 0 at rest and the range on the far side is reduced (F2). A rest pose beyond `PHI_MAX` is clamped to it, and the relative angle is then not 0 at rest (rest 90 gives `steer` 0.787).
- **If fewer than `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` fall in the window at a capture (the buffer was cleared by an app pause, or the run starts within about 0.55 s of launch)**: `neutral_pending` is set and the ball gets `steer = 0` until the first `N_min` valid samples have arrived, whose median becomes `phi0`. A jolt in those frames is filtered by the median, not eliminated (the guard does not apply on this path).
- **If `run_reset` from Menu or `run_resumed` arrives while the state is Acquiring or Unavailable**: `neutral_pending` is set; the capture runs on the first `N_min` valid samples and the filter starts at 0.
- **If a capture event arrives while `neutral_pending` is already true** (for example a `run_reset` while the sensor is invalid, a pause, then `run_resumed`): the accumulator restarts from zero and the samples after the new event decide the neutral (AC-17f).
- **If `run_reset` from Hit arrives while `neutral_stale` is true (the app was backgrounded in Hit)**: it captures like a Menu-to-Play reset; if the player restarts soon after the settle the window may hold too few post-resume samples and the capture goes pending.
- **If the sensor becomes invalid on the frame a run starts (a race: the tap arrived while `valid` was still true)**: the run starts, the driver's `flush()` requests `pause_requested(sensor_lost)` before the settling tick, Run State pauses on it (a pause on the settling tick is accepted), and Resume and Restart stay disabled until `valid` is true; the pause screen offers the Menu. A restart tap while `valid` is already false never reaches Run State (rule 10).
- **If the resume countdown is interrupted by a pause**: `run_resumed` is not emitted (Run State), so no capture happens; the next `run_resumed` captures.
- **If two `run_resumed` events come in quick succession**: each capture is independent and uses the buffer at its own time.
- **If the app is backgrounded**: the buffer is cleared, `neutral_stale` is set and samples are discarded for `SENSOR_RESUME_SETTLE` after the foreground event. With Resume tapped at once, `run_resumed` comes at least 1.0 s later, and rule 14 guarantees `SETTLE + G + W <= 1.0`, so the window holds post-settle samples (AC-24). Only if the countdown is ever shortened below 1.0 s does this stop holding.
- **If the device sleeps**: `Time.get_ticks_usec()` is monotonic and unaffected by `time_scale`, but `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` on Android and `mach_absolute_time` on iOS may not count device sleep (UNVERIFIED, also Run State Open Question 5), so the clock gap after a screen-off can be small. The buffer clear of rule 9 is the protection against a stale window, not the age purge.

**Timing and polling**
- **If the clock stamp does not increase (equal, or going backwards)**: `dt = 0`, no sample is appended, the filter holds and `previous_now` is unchanged.
- **If a hitch is longer than `DT_MAX`**: `dt` is clamped for the filter and for the dropout counter; the ring buffer uses the real clock, so its windows are true time and samples older than `BUFFER_AGE` disappear.
- **If `poll` is called twice in one frame**: the second call carries the same clock stamp, so it appends nothing and uses `dt = 0` (rule 6 forbids it; this is the safe result).
- **If Ball Movement reads `steer` before `poll` in a frame (an ordering bug)**: it reads the previous frame's value, one frame stale; the game-loop ADR fixes the order and an integration test asserts it.
- **If the player taps the pause button and the phone jolts**: nothing special; Run State freezes the run on the same tick and the neutral is recaptured on resume.

**Mapping and settings**
- **If `phi_f` exceeds `FS_eff`**: `u` clamps to +-1 (full lock); there is no wrap and no overshoot.
- **If `sensitivity` from Settings is NaN, INF, zero or negative**: it is replaced by 1; a finite positive value is clamped to `[SENSITIVITY_MIN, SENSITIVITY_MAX]`; each replacement logs one `KNOB_CLAMPED`.
- **If `DEAD_ZONE` is at least 0.2 of `FS_eff`**: it is capped at `0.2 * FS_eff` (F4) at run time, on top of the load-time cap of `0.15 * FS`.
- **If both fallback directions are pressed**: the target is 0.
- **If sensors are live in a debug build**: the fallback input is ignored (`input_source` `SENSOR`).
- **If a capture event arrives while `input_source` is `FALLBACK`**: nothing happens (`neutral_pending` stays false) and `steer` follows the fallback target (AC-48).

## Tuning Knobs

All defaults are guesses until the on-device spike; none comes from keyboard testing. Every knob lives in a `TiltConfig` resource (data-driven), not in code. (`NEUTRAL_LIMIT` was removed in revision 2: a rest pose now passes through up to `PHI_MAX`, F2.)

| Knob | Default | Safe range | Affects | Too low | Too high |
|------|---------|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| `TILT_FULL_SCALE` (FS) | 25 deg | 12-45 | Wrist range for full lock (F4) | Tremor amplified, twitchy steering | Full lock unreachable by twist above about pitch 65 at 25 deg (a 50 deg twist at pitch 60 reads only 22.5 deg) |
| `DEAD_ZONE` (DZ) | 1.5 deg | 0-4 and at most 0.15 * FS | Rest steadiness (F4) | The ball never rests | Mushy fine control |
| `FILTER_TAU` (tau) | 0.05 s | 0.02-0.10 | Smoothness versus lag (F3, F5) | Tremor visible | Lag the player feels |
| `CURVE_EXP` (k) | 1.0 | 1-2 | Mid-range feel (F4); may move to Ball Movement (Open Question 20) | (below 1 is not allowed: amplifies noise) | Stiff mid-range, twitchy near full lock |
| `NEUTRAL_WINDOW` (W) | 0.3 s | 0.15-0.6 and `G + W <= 0.9` | Neutral robustness (F2) | One outlier decides | Window reaches earlier movement |
| `NEUTRAL_GUARD` (G) | 0.25 s | 0.05-0.35 | Skipping the tap jolt (F2); onset of a jolt precedes the handler time by the tap hold | Jolt leaks into the neutral | Window reaches earlier movement |
| `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES` (N_min) | 5 | 3-6 and at most floor(W * `F_MIN`) | Minimum frames in the window and in a pending capture (F2) | Outlier-prone | Always pending at low frame rates |
| `REANCHOR_OFFSET` | 12 deg | 8-16 | How far a steady restart pose must be from both `phi0` and `phi_stop` to recapture (rule 7, F2); a physical posture shift, capped at `REANCHOR_FS_CAP * FS_eff` | Recaptures on small posture shifts | Never recaptures a phone that was moved; leaves up to 0.62 of full lock as a bias at 16 |
| `REANCHOR_SPREAD` | 3.0 deg | 1-6 | Steadiness needed for a recapture at restart (F2) | Tremor prevents any recapture | Any moving hand counts as a rest pose |
| `G_MIN` | 3.0 m/s^2 | 1-7 | Sample validity (rule 4) | Garbage direction in free-fall | Everything invalid (a platform reporting in g units is rejected entirely) |
| `SENSOR_START_TIMEOUT` | 2.0 s | 0.5-5 | Acquiring to Live or `FALLBACK` (states) | False `FALLBACK` on a slow start | Slow start on a phone with no sensor |
| `SENSOR_RESUME_SETTLE` | 0.3 s | 0.1-1.0 and at most `1.0 - G - W` | Samples discarded after an app foreground (rule 9) | A stale vector is accepted | Slow return to Live |
| `DROPOUT_HOLD` | 0.1 s | 0-0.3 | Accumulated clamped poll time of consecutive invalid polls before Unavailable, together with 3 polls (F6) | A blip pauses the run | Ghost steering |
| `FALLBACK_SLEW` | 4 per s | 1-20 | Fallback response (keys and touch) | Sluggish | Effectively digital |
| `SENSITIVITY_MIN` / `MAX` | 0.5 / 2.0 | fixed range of the Settings hook | `sensitivity` clamp (Edge Cases) | Very low sensitivity: `FS_eff` up to 50, harder full lock | High: `FS_eff` halves, dead zone shrinks, twitchy |
| `SENSOR_SIGN` | iOS +1, Android -1 (expected) | +1 or -1 | Direction of the roll (F1) | (set by V-1, not tuned) | (set by V-1, not tuned) |

**Fixed constants (not tuning knobs):** `TAU_FLOOR` = 0.005 s; `BUFFER_AGE` = 1.0 s and buffer capacity 256; `F_MIN` = 20 Hz (lowest supported frame rate); `PHI_MAX` = 70 deg (above it a posture is unsupported); `FS_EFF_MAX` = 50 deg; `DROPOUT_MIN_POLLS` = 3; `REANCHOR_FS_CAP` = 0.5 (the re-anchor offset never exceeds half of `FS_eff`). **Set by the spike, not tuned:** `SENSOR_SIGN` per platform and `L_s`.

**Knob interactions**
- `G + W <= 0.9` and `SETTLE + G + W <= 1.0` (rule 14): inside the shortest `RESUME_COUNTDOWN` (1.0 s, Run State) and the buffer. A restart no longer always captures, so `RESTART_LOCK` does not enter.
- `NEUTRAL_MIN_SAMPLES <= floor(NEUTRAL_WINDOW * F_MIN)` (rule 14).
- `L_eff = max(0, PHI_MAX - FS_eff)` only decides when `POSTURE_UNSUPPORTED` is logged: raising sensitivity shrinks `FS_eff` and widens `L_eff`; lowering it does the opposite.
- `REANCHOR_OFFSET` is in degrees and does not scale with sensitivity (a posture shift is physical); only the fixed cap `REANCHOR_FS_CAP * FS_eff` (half of `FS_eff`) lowers it at high sensitivity, so an inherited neutral cannot leave a saturating bias. With the `phi_stop` comparison a smaller threshold no longer recaptures on a steady death tilt.
- `DEAD_ZONE` and `sensitivity` interact through `FS_eff` (F4 caps the dead zone at `0.2 * FS_eff`).
- The wrist's comfortable range around a captured neutral is not measured (about 60-70 deg is an unverified guess).

**Sources of truth elsewhere:** `DT_MAX` (Run State and registry `dt_max`, 0.1 s), `RESUME_COUNTDOWN` (Run State), `sensitivity` (Settings & Accessibility), speed and angular response of the ball (Ball Movement).
