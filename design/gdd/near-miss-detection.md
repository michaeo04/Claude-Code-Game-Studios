## Overview

Near-Miss Detection watches the same published state Obstacle System already tests for hits — Ball Movement's swept path (`theta`, `s` and their previous-frame values) against each hazard's effective footprint currently bound in Tube Track's window — and answers a different question with it: not "did the ball touch this," but "did the ball pass close enough to it, without touching, that the player earned a reward for the read and the reaction." The instant that's true for a given hazard, it fires one `near_miss_detected(hazard_id, run_id)` event and moves on — no continuous closeness value (Core Rule 7); it decides nothing about what happens next. It exists because Pillar 3 ("every close dodge must feel immediately rewarding") is a promise with no mechanism behind it until something decides, precisely and consistently, *when* a dodge counts as close — and game-concept.md's own predicted player behavior ("players will start taking riskier near-miss lines once they trust their own reflexes, chasing bonus feedback rather than playing maximally safe") only happens if that decision is trustworthy: the same closeness, every time, never generous on a bad frame and never stingy on a good one. It is passive and automatic — the player never presses anything for it, the same way they never press anything to get hit — but unlike Obstacle System's contact test, whose entire job is to stay invisible until the one moment it isn't, this system's whole reason to exist is the payoff on the other side of that same geometry: the thrill is the point, not a side effect. It decides nothing about how a near-miss *feels* (Juice & Feedback owns the white ring pulse, whoosh, FOV punch, and haptic — art bible §2, Mood State 4), whether it's worth points (Scoring & Personal Best's decision), or what counts as a hazard in the first place (Obstacle System's geometry, unchanged here) — it owns exactly one thing: the moment, and how close it was.

## Player Fantasy

**The fantasy.** That split-second where I thread a gap tighter than I needed to, and the game *tells me* I did — a tiny, immediate triumph on top of just surviving (game-concept.md's Core Fantasy: "pure mastery... feeling your reactions get sharper with every run").

**What the player feels.**
- **I know exactly how close that was.** Not a vague "nice dodge" — an unambiguous, immediate signal, read instantly through the same white ring pulse and whoosh every time (art bible §2, Mood State 4), never through a number I have to parse mid-run. The event itself doesn't grade the dodge (Core Rule 7) — the player's own eyes did that the instant they threaded the gap; the game just confirms it happened.
- **The reward tempts me toward the edge, on purpose.** Once I trust that a near-miss is real and repeatable, I start choosing tighter lines for the payoff, not just the minimum safe gap (game-concept.md's own predicted dynamic: "players will start taking riskier near-miss lines once they trust their own reflexes, chasing bonus feedback rather than playing maximally safe"). That's a feature, not scope creep — Pillar 3 exists specifically to make this trade-off exist.
- **It never lies to me, and it never competes with the thing that kills me.** A near-miss cue is unmistakably not a hazard (Pillar 1; art bible §1 principle 3: the cool/white channel only, never the hazard's warm hue or its screen area) and it never fires for a dodge that wasn't actually close, or fails to fire for one that was.

**Feelings to avoid.** A near-miss cue that fires too often to feel special, or too rarely to feel reliable; a reward that reads as a hazard for even a frame (Pillar 1 violation); a "close" that's actually generous (undermines the skill claim) or stingy (a legitimately tight dodge that goes unrewarded feels like the game not seeing what the player just did); any near-miss feedback the player can't tell apart from a different hazard's near-miss a moment later.

**Serves the pillars.** *Pillar 3, Juice on Every Near-Miss*: this system is the trigger Pillar 3's entire promise depends on. *Pillar 1, Instant Readability*: the reward must read as unmistakably not-danger, every time, with zero exceptions — there is no "fair surprise" carve-out here the way Obstacle System has one for hidden hazards.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **Data model.** Near-Miss Detection tracks one lightweight state per currently-bound hazard: `{hazard_id, hit_ever_true, was_in_near_zone}`, keyed by `hazard_id` — which Obstacle System's own Core Rule 1 guarantees is never reused within the same `run_id`, so a stale entry from an earlier hazard can never collide with a new one under the same key. It receives the same raw, unexpanded footprint pieces `(theta_min, theta_max, s_start, s_end)` Obstacle System already defines (Formulas F1), via two new signals proposed on Obstacle System's existing, already-anticipated interface (its Dependencies row: *"Published hazard footprints and `hazard_id`s for pieces currently in the window"*): `hazard_bound(hazard_id, footprint_pieces)`, fired whenever Obstacle System spawns a hazard, and `hazard_released(hazard_id)`, fired whenever it releases one. This resolves Obstacle System's own Open Question 10: Near-Miss Detection wants **raw footprints**, not a pre-derived distance — it owns its own geometry, the same separation of concerns Obstacle System already draws for itself against Tube Track.
2. **The near-miss zone is the hit zone's own expansion, expanded again.** Using Obstacle System's F1 (ball-size expansion) and F2 (swept-rectangle overlap) verbatim — reused, not re-derived — Near-Miss Detection computes each piece's **hit zone** (F1's effective footprint) and a further-expanded **near zone**: `theta_near_min = theta_eff_min - NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN`, `theta_near_max = theta_eff_max + NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN`, `s_near_start = s_eff_start - NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN`, `s_near_end = s_eff_end + NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN` (Formulas F1-NM). A near-miss can only ever be reported for a frame where the swept path overlaps the near zone but **not** the hit zone — by construction, the two zones can never both be true for a real near-miss, since the near zone strictly contains the hit zone.
3. **Swept, not sampled.** Ball Movement's published state already includes `theta_prev`/`s_prev` for Obstacle System's own use; this GDD proposes extending the same publication to Near-Miss Detection (a cross-file addition to `ball-movement.md`'s Dependencies table, not a new computation — Ball Movement already holds these values). Near-Miss Detection reuses Obstacle System's F2 swept-rectangle test verbatim against both zones every frame, for the same reason Obstacle System's own hit test is swept and not point-sampled: a point-sampled test could silently miss the fastest, tightest passes — exactly the dodges Player Fantasy most wants rewarded.
4. **A hit always overrides a near-miss for the same hazard.** Near-Miss Detection listens to Obstacle System's `hit_reported(hazard_id, run_id)` purely as a read signal (never a game-state mutation) and sets `hit_ever_true = true` for that hazard_id the instant it's received. A hazard that is ever hit during its bound lifetime never produces a `near_miss_detected`, even if an earlier frame in the same pass was a clean graze — the player's own actual outcome (survived vs. hit) is always the final word. **This is a deliberate choice, not an oversight, including for the multi-piece case** (Rule 6): a player who cleanly grazes one piece of a Spike cluster or Double Gate and then clips a *different* piece of the same hazard gets no near-miss acknowledgment for the earlier graze, on top of the death. Rewarding the graze anyway would mean telling the player "you read that correctly" about a hazard that, taken as a whole (its own `hazard_id`, the actual unit Obstacle System and this system both key on), killed them a moment later — a reward the player's own outcome directly contradicts. Because the reward is edge-triggered on exit (Rule 5), nothing is ever visually or audibly *revoked* — the near-miss juice for that approach simply never fires, the same as a graze that was never close enough in the first place. Whether this reads as fair or as an unacknowledged near-attempt on a multi-piece hazard specifically is exactly what AC-11's added row below is scoped to check. **Canonical same-tick order:** within any single tick, an incoming `hit_reported` is applied (setting `hit_ever_true`) before that same tick's exit-edge check (Rule 5) runs — this is a stated processing order, not a coincidence of implementation, so a hit and an exit-edge condition landing on the same tick always resolve to "no near-miss," regardless of which one the driver happens to deliver first at the call-site level.
5. **One report per hazard, on exit, not on entry.** While `hit_ever_true` is false, each frame's near-zone-but-not-hit-zone test (Rule 2-3) sets `was_in_near_zone = true`. The instant that test goes from true to false (the ball has swept past) **or** the hazard is released (`hazard_released`), whichever comes first: if `was_in_near_zone` is true and `hit_ever_true` is false, send exactly one `near_miss_detected(hazard_id, run_id)`. Waiting for the exit edge (not firing on the first near-zone frame) is what lets Rule 4's override still apply for every frame of the approach, right up to the last one.
6. **One report per hazard, not per piece.** A multi-piece hazard sharing one `hazard_id` (Double Gate, a Spike cluster) is tracked as a single state entry keyed by `hazard_id`; if any piece's near zone is active this frame, the hazard-level `was_in_near_zone` is true — mirroring Obstacle System's own Core Rule 4 (one `hit_reported` per hazard, not per piece).
7. **No intensity, no closeness score.** `near_miss_detected` carries only `hazard_id` and `run_id` — no continuous "how close" value. The art bible's Mood State 4 already specifies a fixed-intensity effect; scaling by margin is not asked for by any downstream GDD today and would require deriving an unproven distance-like metric from an AABB test that doesn't naturally produce one. Revisit only if Juice & Feedback, once authored, asks for it (Open Questions).
8. **Deterministic, level-triggered inputs, edge-triggered output.** Near-Miss Detection uses no randomness. It runs its per-frame test in every phase, the same way Obstacle System's hit test does (Ball Movement's published state already collapses to a stationary check when frozen), but its *output* is edge-triggered (Rule 5), not level-triggered like `hit_reported` — a near-miss is a single moment, not a persisting condition Run State needs to keep hearing about through a pause.
9. **No side effects beyond `near_miss_detected`.** Near-Miss Detection sends no requests to Ball Movement, Obstacle System, or Run State; it reads `theta`/`s`/`theta_prev`/`s_prev` from Ball Movement, hazard footprints/`hazard_id`s and `hit_reported` from Obstacle System, and `run_id` from the last `run_reset` it saw (Run State & Restart, Hard dependency for `run_id` only — a new dependency edge, the same pattern Obstacle System already uses). It owns no physics body and makes no judgment about score or feel.

### States and Transitions

Near-Miss Detection has no phase of its own; it is a pure follower of Obstacle System's hazard lifecycle. It needs no window-binding logic of its own, because clearing on `hazard_released` (which Obstacle System already fires for every bound hazard on a `window_primed` reset) naturally clears this system's state on every `run_reset` too.

| Situation | Event | Near-Miss Detection does |
|-----------|-------|---------------------------|
| Boot, Menu | none | No per-hazard state held; nothing queried |
| A hazard spawns | `hazard_bound(hazard_id, footprint_pieces)` | Creates a fresh `{hit_ever_true: false, was_in_near_zone: false}` entry |
| A hazard is released (recycle, or a `run_reset`-driven `window_primed`) | `hazard_released(hazard_id)` | If `was_in_near_zone` and not `hit_ever_true`: sends `near_miss_detected` (Rule 5); discards the entry either way |
| Any bound hazard, any phase | every tick | Runs the swept near-zone/hit-zone test (Rules 2-3) |
| A bound hazard is hit | `hit_reported(hazard_id, run_id)` | Sets `hit_ever_true = true` for that hazard_id (Rule 4) |
| A bound hazard's near-zone test goes true -> false, `hit_ever_true` still false | every tick | Sends `near_miss_detected(hazard_id, run_id)` (Rule 5), then clears `was_in_near_zone` |

## Formulas

All worked examples use the registry defaults (R = 3.0, D = 0.8, v_max = 25, BALL_HALF_ANGLE = 0.1179 rad, w = 0.2358 rad, GAP_MARGIN = 2.5) plus two new tuning knobs this GDD proposes: `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` = 1.0 and `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF` = 1.0 (Tuning Knobs). F1-NM is the only new formula; F2 is Obstacle System's own swept-rectangle test, reused verbatim and invoked twice. Deliberately, neither new margin is a function of `v_max`: unlike F4b's `HIDDEN_SPAN_MIN_S` or F5's `S_MIN_SPACING` — which are wall-clock *fairness* floors that must not thin as the run's speed ramps — a near-miss margin is a *physical closeness* measure. If it scaled with `v_max` it would get easier to trigger later in a run purely because the ball is faster, not because the dodge was any tighter; anchoring both margins to the ball's own fixed geometry (`BALL_HALF_ANGLE`, `D`) keeps "how close counts as close" constant for the whole run.

**The time budget this buys is not constant, even though the space is.** `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN` (0.4 u at defaults) is a fixed *distance*; the *time* a fast-moving ball spends inside that distance is `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN / v_max`, which shrinks as the run's speed ramps. At `v_max` = 25 (default) this is `0.4 / 25` = 16 ms — about one frame at 60 Hz. This does not threaten F2's own mutual-exclusion proof (that proof is purely geometric and holds regardless of speed), but it is the real number that decides whether the near-zone/hit-zone distinction survives contact with a real device: at 16 ms, a single dropped frame or a small amount of input/render latency could plausibly consume the entire along-track near-miss window at the run's fastest speed. This is flagged for the on-device spike (Open Questions) rather than resolved here, since it depends on measured frame timing this GDD does not have.

**F1-NM. Near-zone expansion (the hit zone's own expansion, expanded again)**

```
theta_near_min = theta_eff_min - NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN
theta_near_max = theta_eff_max + NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN
s_near_start   = s_eff_start - NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN
s_near_end     = s_eff_end + NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN

NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN = NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF * BALL_HALF_ANGLE     (BALL_HALF_ANGLE: registry / Tube Track F6 / Obstacle System F1 — not rederived here)
NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN     = NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF * (D / 2)
```

`theta_eff_min/max` and `s_eff_start/end` are Obstacle System's F1 output, taken verbatim — Near-Miss Detection performs no ball-size expansion of its own, only a second expansion on top of it. The two coefficients are the actual tuning knobs (dimensionless), not the margins themselves, for the same reason Obstacle System exposes `GAP_MARGIN` rather than a bare `GAP_MIN`: the margin should scale automatically if `R`/`D` change, not be re-tuned by hand every time the ball's geometry does.

**Why these two base units, not a fresh guess.** `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` and `D/2` are not new geometric ideas — they are exactly the two terms F1 already used once to turn a raw footprint into a hit zone. F1-NM is a second, identical application of the same expansion: "near" is defined as *one more application of the ball's own footprint margin* beyond actual contact, on each axis independently. This was chosen over an alternative — sourcing the near-zone margin from `GAP_MARGIN`/`w` (the placement-validation safety margin) — deliberately: `GAP_MARGIN` describes how hazards are *authored to be spaced*, a level-design policy that could change per hazard type or difficulty tier; a near-miss's definition of "close" should not silently change meaning depending on how tightly a given level happens to pack its hazards. Tying it to the ball's own constant geometry keeps "close" meaning the same physical thing everywhere. `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` and `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF` default to 1.0 (safe range 0.5-1.5 each) — large enough to be reliably distinguishable from the hit zone at any legal `BALL_LAG_TAU`/latency (a coefficient below ~0.5 risks a near zone too thin to register consistently across a dropped-frame gap), small enough that it never approaches the clearance F3's `GAP_MIN` guarantees (proof in F3-NM below) even at both knobs' safe-range ceilings.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `theta_eff_min`, `theta_eff_max`, `s_eff_start`, `s_eff_end` | float | Obstacle System F1 output | the hit zone, taken verbatim |
| `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` | float | `[0.5, 1.5]` (proposed default 1.0) | dimensionless multiplier on `BALL_HALF_ANGLE`; the real tuning knob |
| `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF` | float | `[0.5, 1.5]` (proposed default 1.0) | dimensionless multiplier on `D/2`; the real tuning knob |
| `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN` | float | derived, `> 0` strictly | additional angular buffer beyond the hit zone, each side |
| `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN` | float | derived, `> 0` strictly | additional along-track buffer beyond the hit zone, each side |
| `theta_near_min`, `theta_near_max` | float | `theta_near_min < theta_eff_min`, `theta_near_max > theta_eff_max` | the near zone's angular bounds |
| `s_near_start`, `s_near_end` | float | `s_near_start < s_eff_start`, `s_near_end > s_eff_end` | the near zone's distance bounds |

**Output range:** `theta_near_max - theta_near_min = (theta_eff_max - theta_eff_min) + 2*NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN`, strictly wider than the hit zone on both sides (never equal — both coefficients are bounded away from 0 by the proposed safe range's floor of 0.5). `s_near_end - s_near_start = (s_eff_end - s_eff_start) + 2*NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN`, likewise strictly wider, unbounded above. Whether the near zone can grow wide enough to reach `2*PI` or touch a different hazard's zone is F3-NM's concern, not this formula's — F1-NM only ever expands one piece's own bounds.

**Example** (reusing Obstacle System F1's own worked piece for continuity: raw `theta_min = -0.3, theta_max = 0.3`, `s_start = 100, s_end = 101.5`, whose hit zone is `theta_eff = [-0.4179, 0.4179]`, `s_eff = [99.6, 101.9]`). At defaults, `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN = 1.0 * 0.1179 = 0.1179` rad, `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN = 1.0 * 0.4 = 0.4` u: `theta_near_min = -0.5358, theta_near_max = 0.5358` (near-zone angular span 1.0716 rad, 0.2358 rad — exactly `w` — wider than the hit zone's 0.8358 rad); `s_near_start = 99.2, s_near_end = 102.3` (near-zone span 3.1 u, 0.8 u — exactly `D` — wider than the hit zone's 2.3 u).

**The angular margin in world units, not just radians.** `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN` (0.1179 rad at defaults) is an arc length of `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN * (R + D/2)` = `0.1179 * 3.4` = 0.354 u at the ball's own riding radius — about `0.44` ball diameters (`D` = 0.8) of genuine clear air past the hit zone, on that one side. This is one side's contribution only; the near zone's *total* extra width beyond the hit zone (both sides combined) is twice this. Recording the number this way, not only in radians, is what makes "how close is close" auditable by whoever eventually judges whether it *feels* close on a real device (NM-1) — a designer reading "0.1179 rad" cannot easily tell if that is generous or stingy without converting it first.

**F2 (reused). Swept-rectangle overlap test, invoked twice per piece per frame**

```
dtheta, swept_start, swept_width           computed once per frame (Obstacle System F2) — shared by both invocations below

HIT_ZONE  = arc_overlap(swept_start, swept_width, theta_eff_min,  theta_eff_max  - theta_eff_min)  and (s_prev <= s_eff_end)  and (s >= s_eff_start)
NEAR_ZONE = arc_overlap(swept_start, swept_width, theta_near_min, theta_near_max - theta_near_min) and (s_prev <= s_near_end) and (s >= s_near_start)

NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE = NEAR_ZONE and not HIT_ZONE
```

Obstacle System's `arc_overlap` and the `s_hit` per-axis test (F2) are called twice per bound piece per tick, once against each zone's bounds — never re-derived, only re-parameterized. `dtheta`/`swept_start`/`swept_width` depend only on `(theta_prev, theta)`, so they are computed once per frame and shared by both calls; only the four effective-bounds arguments differ.

**The "near zone strictly contains hit zone" invariant.** Because `theta_near_min <= theta_eff_min <= theta_eff_max <= theta_near_max` and `s_near_start <= s_eff_start <= s_eff_end <= s_near_end` (F1-NM's output range, both strict since both coefficients are bounded away from 0), any swept path/piece pair with `HIT_ZONE = true` necessarily also has `NEAR_ZONE = true` (a wider box's per-axis tests can only be true more often, never less, than a narrower box's). So `HIT_ZONE => NEAR_ZONE` always, and `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE` and `HIT_ZONE` can never both be true on the same frame — the mutual exclusion Core Rule 2 asserts is a direct consequence of F1-NM's output range, not a separate runtime check. **Sufficiency check on the strict inequality:** `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN > 0` and `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN > 0` (both, independently) is necessary for each axis to contribute its own genuine "near but not hit" region: if `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN` were 0, the two zones' theta-bounds would be numerically identical, so `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE` could only ever fire from a graze past a hazard's along-track (front/back) edge, never its angular (side) edge — a real but silently narrowed detector, not a broken one, since strict containment as a 2D box only requires *one* margin to be positive. The design intent (Player Fantasy: "I know exactly how close that was," on either a side-graze or an edge-graze) needs *both* margins independently `> 0`, which the proposed safe range (floor 0.5, never 0) guarantees. **No boundary case makes them equal or degenerate at the proposed defaults or safe range** — both floors are bounded away from 0 by construction, so this is a load-bearing invariant of the *tuning-knob range*, not just the shipped defaults; a config validator rejects `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF <= 0` or `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF <= 0` at load with `NEAR_MISS_MARGIN_NONPOSITIVE` (Edge Cases), the same way `BallConfig` is validated elsewhere.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|--------------|
| `HIT_ZONE`, `NEAR_ZONE` | bool | — | Obstacle System F2, evaluated against the hit zone and near zone respectively |
| `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE` | bool | — | true iff the swept path is inside the near zone but not the hit zone, this frame |

**Output range:** boolean; `HIT_ZONE => NEAR_ZONE` always holds (proof above), so `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE` and `HIT_ZONE` are mutually exclusive by construction, every frame, for every legal coefficient value.

**Example** (same piece as F1-NM's worked example). Take a frame where `theta_prev = -0.5, theta = -0.45` (a graze past the side, `dtheta = 0.05`), `s_prev = 100.5, s = 100.6` (well inside the along-track span). `theta_eff = [-0.4179, 0.4179]`: swept arc `[-0.5, -0.45]` does not reach `-0.4179`, so `HIT_ZONE`'s theta term is false, `HIT_ZONE = false`. `theta_near = [-0.5358, 0.5358]`: the swept arc `[-0.5, -0.45]` is fully inside it, so `NEAR_ZONE`'s theta term is true; `s` is inside both `s_eff` and `s_near`, so `NEAR_ZONE = true`. `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE = true and not false = true` — a clean angular graze.

**F3-NM. Joint-extreme check — near zones vs. adjacent hazards, and the 2*PI wraparound**

**The clean, geometry-independent result.** Let `k = NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF`. Two angularly-adjacent hazard pieces separated by an open arc of exactly `GAP_MIN` (F3's own floor, the worst legal case) each extend their near zone `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_MARGIN = k * BALL_HALF_ANGLE` into that gap from their own side. Total consumed from the gap by both near zones: `2 * k * BALL_HALF_ANGLE`. Since `GAP_MIN = GAP_MARGIN * w = GAP_MARGIN * 2 * BALL_HALF_ANGLE` (F3), the **fraction of the minimum legal gap the two near zones can ever consume together is**:

```
fraction_consumed = (2 * k * BALL_HALF_ANGLE) / GAP_MIN = k / GAP_MARGIN
```

— independent of `R`, `D`, and `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` entirely (they cancel). The two near zones cannot touch or overlap as long as `fraction_consumed < 1`, i.e. `k < GAP_MARGIN` at GAP_MARGIN's own worst (smallest) legal value.

**Numeric check.** At `k` = 1.0 (proposed default) and `GAP_MARGIN` = 2.5 (shipped default): `fraction_consumed = 1.0/2.5 = 0.4` — 60% of the minimum legal gap remains genuinely open (neither hazard's near zone). At `k` = 1.5 (proposed safe-range ceiling) against `GAP_MARGIN`'s own safe-range floor (2.0, corrected 2026-09-28 to match its Tuning Knobs table and the registry): `fraction_consumed = 1.5/2.0 = 0.75` — still `< 1` (25% remains), so no overlap even at both knobs' worst legal corner together.

**Wraparound (a single very-wide hazard, e.g. Near-Ring, is the same case in disguise).** A Near-Ring's own single gap is exactly the `open(s0)` F3 validates, so the largest legal single-piece effective footprint is `2*PI - GAP_MIN`. Its near zone's width is `(2*PI - GAP_MIN) + 2*k*BALL_HALF_ANGLE = 2*PI - GAP_MIN*(1 - k/GAP_MARGIN)`. Since `k/GAP_MARGIN < 1` (shown above), the subtracted term is strictly positive, so the near zone's own width is **strictly less than `2*PI`** — it never wraps past the full circumference, for the same reason and the same numeric margin as the two-hazard case.

**The one case this does not cover by construction: 3+ pieces sharing a critical `s0`.** F3's own validator checks `occupied(s0) = sum(...)` and requires `open(s0) >= GAP_MIN` in aggregate — it does not require every individual pairwise gap between adjacent pieces to itself reach `GAP_MIN` when three or more pieces share one critical `s0` and the open arc is split into multiple smaller gaps. In that (unusual, but not forbidden by F3's literal text) topology, a single pairwise gap could be narrower than `GAP_MIN`, and the proof above (which assumes one contiguous `GAP_MIN` gap between exactly two neighbors) would not apply — two near zones could then overlap. Rather than reopening Obstacle System's Approved F3, this is guarded by a new Near-Miss-Detection-owned preflight check, run at the same offline preflight stage over the whole preflighted content library (the same scope as Obstacle System's own `HAZARD_OVERLAP`, not window-local): sort every piece active at each of F3's own critical `s0` points by `theta_eff` center, and for each pair of **angularly-adjacent neighbors in that sorted order** (including the wraparound pair closing the circle, exactly the ordering AC-9's worked example uses), verify their *near* zones (not hit zones) do not overlap in theta; reject with a new stable code, `NEAR_ZONE_OVERLAP`, naming the `s0` and the offending pair, on violation (Edge Cases, Acceptance Criteria). This never fires for the common single-gap topology (proven safe above) and only guards the rarer multi-piece case.

**A distinct, out-of-scope-by-design case: two hazards with no shared critical `s0` at all.** The check above only re-inspects pairs at critical `s0` points derived from Obstacle System's own F3 validator, which are anchored to *hit-zone* boundaries. Because `NEAR_MISS_S_MARGIN` expands a piece's zone along `s` beyond its hit-zone boundary, two hazards that are sequential along the track (never sharing any hit-zone-derived `s0`, e.g. the same angular band, far apart in `s`) can still end up with overlapping *near* zones — but no critical `s0` exists at which the check above would ever examine that pair, so it is never flagged. This is accepted, not a gap in the check: `NEAR_ZONE_OVERLAP`'s job is to prevent a false "no safe gap" reading in `theta` at a shared point in `s` (Obstacle System's own concern), and two near-zones overlapping in `s` alone, at different points along the track, cannot close a safe line — the ball only ever needs to be somewhere safe in `theta` at whatever `s` it currently occupies, and nothing here removes that. The real, named cost is purely presentational: two `near_miss_detected` events landing within a few frames of each other for two different, sequential hazards — the exact "feedback the player can't tell apart from a different hazard's near-miss a moment later" case this GDD's own Player Fantasy lists as a feeling to avoid (Feelings to Avoid). This is handed to Juice & Feedback as a named requirement (Open Questions) rather than solved here: any two `near_miss_detected` events landing within a short window of each other should be visually coalesced (e.g. a single reinforced pulse, not two independent ones), so a legitimate double-graze never reads as a glitchy double-fire.

No additional formula is needed for Core Rule 5's edge-triggered output. The exit-edge detection (`was_in_near_zone` true -> false, or `hazard_released`, gated by `hit_ever_true`) is a boolean state transition already fully specified by Core Rule 5 and the States and Transitions table; it consumes only the two booleans F2-reused already computes (`HIT_ZONE`, `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE`) and introduces no new numeric parameter.

## Edge Cases

- **If a hazard is released the same tick it would have exited the near zone anyway**: no double-count — `hazard_released` is checked first each tick per the States and Transitions table; if `was_in_near_zone` is true and `hit_ever_true` is false at release, exactly one `near_miss_detected` fires, whether the release was triggered by normal window recycling or coincided with the natural exit edge.
- **If `hit_reported` and the near-zone exit edge would both resolve on the same tick**: `hit_ever_true` is set the instant `hit_reported` arrives (Core Rule 4), and the exit-edge check (Core Rule 5) reads it after — so a hit arriving on the very last near-zone frame still correctly suppresses the near-miss, regardless of arrival order within the tick.
- **If `dt_eff` is 0 this frame (a settling tick, Paused, Hit, or any frame nothing moved)**: `theta_prev == theta` and `s_prev == s` (Ball Movement's own no-op contract, the same one Obstacle System relies on), so both the hit-zone and near-zone swept tests degenerate to stationary point-in-footprint checks — no special-casing needed, matching Obstacle System's own Core Rule 3 mechanism.
- **If a hidden-side hazard (Obstacle System's F4/Core Rule 8) is currently barred from shipping at all**: moot for now — no hidden-side content exists to near-miss. Once Open Questions 2/15 there resolve and hidden-side content ships, a near-miss on a hazard the player never saw coming until mid-graze is still detected identically (this system has no visibility awareness of its own); whether that reads as a fair reward or a confusing one is a playtest question for that later content, not a defect here.
- **If Ball Movement ever publishes a non-finite `theta` or `s`**: treated as a no-op frame (holds the last known-good swept endpoint), the same guard Obstacle System applies to its own hit test; never propagates NaN into `NEAR_MISS_CANDIDATE`.
- **`NEAR_ZONE_OVERLAP` (F3-NM's guarded gap): if the offline preflight finds two hazard pieces angularly adjacent at a critical `s0` whose *near* zones would overlap** (only reachable when 3+ pieces share one critical `s0`, per F3-NM): rejected at the same preflight stage Obstacle System's own `NO_SAFE_GAP`/`HAZARD_OVERLAP` run at, checked across the whole preflighted content library (not just one segment or its neighbors — the same scope `HAZARD_OVERLAP` uses), with a structured record (the `s0`, the offending piece pair) — this never blocks the two-piece topology F3-NM proves safe by construction.
- **If `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` or `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF` is 0 or negative at config load**: rejected with `NEAR_MISS_MARGIN_NONPOSITIVE`, one per offending value — F1-NM's strict-containment invariant (F2's own proof) requires both margins positive, so this is a correctness gate, not a style preference.
- **If two different hazard instances' near zones legitimately overlap in `s` but not `theta` (different lanes, same distance range)**: not an error — `NEAR_ZONE_OVERLAP` only checks angular adjacency at a shared critical `s0`, the same scope F3's own `NO_SAFE_GAP`/`HAZARD_OVERLAP` use.
- **If a hazard's `hazard_bound` and `hazard_released` arrive for the same `hazard_id` within the same tick (a same-tick spawn-then-immediate-recycle, not expected in normal play but not assumed impossible)**: the state entry is created then immediately torn down in the same order Obstacle System's own window-primed re-prime already handles (release-before-repopulate); no `near_miss_detected` can fire since `was_in_near_zone` starts false and no test runs in between.

## Tuning Knobs

| Knob | Default | Safe range | Affects | Too low | Too high |
|------|---------|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` | 1.0 | 0.5-1.5 | Angular width of the near zone beyond the hit zone (F1-NM) | Below ~0.5, the near zone may be too thin to register consistently across a dropped-frame gap (`BALL_LAG_TAU`/latency) | Approaches (but per F3-NM, never reaches) overlapping an adjacent hazard's near zone; a "near-miss" that fires for a dodge that wasn't actually tight |
| `NEAR_MISS_S_COEFF` | 1.0 | 0.5-1.5 | Along-track width of the near zone beyond the hit zone (F1-NM) | Same risk as above, along `s` instead of `theta` | Same risk as above, along `s` instead of `theta` |

**Fixed constants (not tuning knobs):** none new — `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` and `D` are Ball Movement's/Tube Track's own constants, referenced here, not owned.

**Knob interactions**
- Both coefficients scale automatically with `R`/`D`/`GAP_MARGIN` changes (they multiply `BALL_HALF_ANGLE` and `D/2`, not fixed values), so a change to the ball's geometry or the placement-safety margin never silently desyncs "how close counts as close" from "what layouts are legal" — F3-NM's proof (`fraction_consumed = NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF / GAP_MARGIN < 1`) holds across the full safe range of both, not just at defaults.
- `GAP_MARGIN` (Obstacle System) indirectly bounds how high `NEAR_MISS_ANGLE_COEFF` could safely go if it were ever raised past 1.5 — F3-NM's proof is what should be re-checked before either knob's safe range is widened, not a fresh guess.

**Sources of truth elsewhere:** `BALL_HALF_ANGLE`, `w` (Tube Track F6 / Obstacle System F1); `D`, `R` (Ball Movement, Tube Track); `GAP_MARGIN`, `GAP_MIN` (Obstacle System F3).
