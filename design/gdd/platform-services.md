# Platform Services

> **Status**: Approved, pending the device spike (first `/design-review` 2026-09-21 was NEEDS REVISION with 9 blocking items; all 9 applied the same day and the user accepted the revision without a further full document round; the next evidence is the `PlatformCore` harness and the device spike PS-1..PS-12, of which PS-1..PS-4 block the first playable)
> **Author**: user + agents
> **Last Updated**: 2026-09-21
> **Supports Pillars**: Pillar 2 (Fair but Merciless Difficulty), by keeping app interruptions out of deaths; Pillar 4 (One-Thumb Simplicity), by keeping the phone a reliable controller

## Overview

Platform Services is the Core-layer system that owns what the game needs from the phone's operating system, so that no other system touches the OS directly. It turns raw OS events into a few game-level signals: the app going to the background or coming back, a phone call or other interruption, and the Android Back button (provisional signals `app_interrupted`, `app_returned`, `app_backgrounded`, `app_foregrounded` and `back_pressed`, consumed by Tilt Input and Run State). It also owns the platform settings the other systems assume rather than check: the locked portrait orientation, the Android sensor project settings that Tilt Input needs, the immersive-mode and Picture-in-Picture export settings, the setting that keeps the screen awake during a tilt-only run, and the display facts Camera and HUD read (viewport size, safe area, refresh rate). A haptic call for Juice & Feedback and the lifecycle hook Save & Persistence uses before the OS can terminate the app are also expected here (both provisional, to be settled in Detailed Design). It is fully automatic: the player never interacts with it and it has no UI. It exists because a phone game is interrupted constantly, and a call, a swipe home or a locked screen must never cost the player a run (Pillar 2), and a phone that rotates, dims or drops its sensors must never make the one control unreliable (Pillar 4). It decides nothing about what the game does in response: Run State decides to pause, Tilt Input decides how a lost sensor is handled, Juice & Feedback decides which haptic patterns play, and Save & Persistence decides what is written. Which devices are supported and what thermal or load budget applies are not decided here (Technical Setup, Open Questions).

## Player Fantasy

**The fantasy.** None of its own. The player never sees this system, and its best result is that they never think of it. What they feel is what it makes possible: the phone is a reliable controller, and the game survives real life.

**What the player feels (indirectly).**
- **An interruption never costs me a run, where the OS tells the game about it.** A call, a swipe home or a locked screen leaves the run exactly where it was. This is Run State's "the run only ends when I lose"; Platform Services is how the game learns that the interruption happened. The limits are stated under "Where the promise holds".
- **The phone never fights me.** The screen stays in portrait however the phone is held or turned (*Pillar 4: "the game is playable one-handed in any normal holding posture: seated, standing, reclined, or commuting"*), the screen does not dim or lock while I am tilting (a tilt-only game gives the OS no touch to see), the motion sensors are on before the first tap, and nothing interrupts a run with a prompt (that no permission prompt appears is UNVERIFIED until a device test, Open Question).
- **A near-miss can be felt in the hand, and a hit always is.** A short haptic pulse joins the white-juice channel of the art bible ("ring motion, whoosh, haptic"; *Pillar 3: "every close dodge must feel immediately rewarding"*). The death pulse is never lost to a near-miss pulse that came just before it (Core Rules 6). It is an extra channel, never the only cue: what happened must be readable without it (*Pillar 1*), and Juice & Feedback owns the final feel.
- **Back and home behave as I expect during a run.** Android Back and a swipe home pause a run in progress; they never quit the game in the middle of a run (Run State rule 11). What Back does in the menus is Menus & Screen Flow's decision.

**Feelings to avoid.** A run lost to a phone call; the layout turning or jumping under the thumb; the screen dimming or locking mid-run; a permission prompt in the middle of play; a vibration that is long, strong, or fires when nothing happened; a death with no haptic because a near-miss pulse played just before; the game quitting on a stray Back press.

**Where the promise holds.** Only where the OS tells the app what is happening.
- If the OS terminates the app while it is in the background, the run is lost (Run State Open Question 6: nothing is saved mid-run in the MVP).
- **On Android a pulled-down notification shade or a heads-up call is not expected to emit any lifecycle event** (UNVERIFIED, PS-1), so the run may continue under it. The mitigation is sticky immersive mode (Core Rules 8): the first swipe from the top edge only shows transient system bars and only a second swipe pulls the shade. Until PS-1 measures it, the game does not promise that a shade never costs a run. A native focus hook is a post-spike escalation to the technical-director (Open Question 2). On iOS, Control Center and banners do pause the run.
- A phone with no usable motion sensor is Tilt Input's touch fallback, not this system's job.

**Serves the pillars.** *Pillar 2, "every death traces back to the player's own choice or reaction, never to randomness or bad luck"*: an interruption the OS reports is never a death, and a dimmed screen is prevented. Pillar 4 as quoted above. Pillar 3 and Pillar 1 through the haptic channel.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

1. **One owner of OS-facing calls.** Only Platform Services handles the application lifecycle notifications (`NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_FOCUS_IN/OUT`, `_PAUSED/_RESUMED`) and `NOTIFICATION_WM_GO_BACK_REQUEST`, calls `Input.vibrate_handheld`, calls `DisplayServer.screen_set_keep_on`, reads the `DisplayServer` display facts, and reads `ProjectSettings` for the boot check. No other system does; a CI lint enforces it (AC-12). Motion sensors are the exception and belong to Tilt Input (its rule 1).
2. **Shape.** `PlatformCore` (`RefCounted`, no Node, no engine calls) holds the logic. `PlatformMath` (static functions, no state) holds the pure functions: `haptic_gate`, `effective`, `interval_us`, `fps_eff`, `frame_time` and `fis_for_platform`. `RateLimitedLog` (`RefCounted`) wraps a log sink with the log rate limit (Tuning Knobs, fixed constants) on the injected clock. A thin `PlatformServices` node in the main scene forwards notifications to the core, calls the OS and reads `ProjectSettings`. `HapticsConfig` (a `Resource`) holds the haptic values. `PlatformSettings` is the manifest of required project settings (rule 8) with the pure function `mismatches(manifest, read)`. No autoload: the composition root creates the node and passes it to the systems that need it (coding standards: dependency injection over singletons).
3. **Lifecycle model.** The core keeps two flags, `focused` (starts true) and `paused` (starts false), and takes a construction parameter `focus_implies_suspend` (`FIS`: true on Android, false on iOS). `on_focus_out()` clears `focused`, `on_focus_in()` sets it. **Under `FIS` true, `on_paused()` and `on_resumed()` are ignored** (they never change `paused`): on Android `onPause` is the only source of `FOCUS_OUT` (source-verified, 4.7.2) and is also when the OS unregisters the sensors and pauses rendering, while `APPLICATION_PAUSED/_RESUMED` are called only from the GL renderer and may never fire under Vulkan (Vulkan path UNVERIFIED); when they do fire they add nothing, and a late pair arriving after `FOCUS_IN` would otherwise cause a phantom second interruption, a second Save flush and a second Tilt Input buffer clear. Under `FIS` false (iOS), `on_paused()` sets `paused` and `on_resumed()` clears it. Derived: `attentive = focused and not paused`; `suspended = paused or (FIS and not focused)`. On iOS `FOCUS_OUT` also fires for Control Center, banners and call interruptions with nothing suspended, and `PAUSED` fires only on a true background (source-verified). So the model must not depend on the order or the presence of the four notifications. `fis_for_platform(os_name)` is `true` for `"Android"` and `false` for `"iOS"` and for every other name (desktop, editor and web builds; on a desktop build only `FOCUS_OUT/IN` matter).
4. **Signals, on edges only.** After every event the core re-evaluates and emits: `app_interrupted` when `attentive` turns false; `app_backgrounded` when `suspended` turns true; `app_foregrounded` when `suspended` turns false; `app_returned` when `attentive` turns true. On losing, `app_interrupted` comes before `app_backgrounded`; on regaining, `app_foregrounded` comes before `app_returned`. A repeated notification changes nothing and emits nothing (one `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` debug log); under `FIS` true a `PAUSED/RESUMED` pair is ignored whatever its order. Nothing is emitted at construction. Signals are emitted synchronously inside the notification handler, which the engine delivers between frames (UNVERIFIED on Android, whose lifecycle calls originate on another thread), so a receiver should not run inside another system's tick (Run State rule 3 rejects a request sent from inside one).
5. **Back.** `NOTIFICATION_WM_GO_BACK_REQUEST` becomes the signal `back_pressed`, one per notification (source-verified: it reaches every node through the scene tree). Platform Services decides nothing about what Back means: Run State's adapter turns it into `pause_requested(back)` in Running and Resuming (Run State rule 11), Menus & Screen Flow decides every other phase (Run State Open Question 16). `application/config/quit_on_go_back` must be false, otherwise the engine quits before the game sees it; the manifest lint (rule 8) is the gate and the node also sets `get_tree().quit_on_go_back = false` at boot. Platform Services exposes `quit()` for Menus and never quits on its own. iOS has no Back. **Android 16 with target SDK 36:** the legacy back path may not be delivered unless the engine registers an `OnBackInvokedCallback` (UNVERIFIED for the 4.7.2 export template). If `WM_GO_BACK_REQUEST` never arrives there, the system performs back-to-home, which the game sees as `FOCUS_OUT` and pauses the run (no run is lost), but `back_pressed` is never available to Menus; PS-4 must run on Android 16 with target SDK 36 and inspect the generated manifest, and a missing `back_pressed` there is a release blocker.
6. **Haptics.** `haptic(kind)` with kinds `NEAR_MISS`, `HIT` and `UI_TAP` (provisional; Juice & Feedback may add kinds through the config). `HapticsConfig` holds, per kind, `duration_ms`, `amplitude` and `priority` (an integer; strictly higher preempts, see F2), plus a global minimum interval. A call is evaluated in this order, and the first matching cause drops it and increments that cause's drop counter: `UNKNOWN_KIND` (the kind is not in the config), `DISABLED` (haptics disabled through a setter fed by Settings & Accessibility), `NOT_ATTENTIVE` (the app is not `attentive`), `ZERO_DURATION` (the kind's effective duration is 0 or less), `THROTTLED` (the gate of F2 is closed and the kind's priority is not strictly higher than the last played pulse's). Otherwise it calls `Input.vibrate_handheld(duration_ms, amplitude)`. The core exposes `haptic_drops(cause)` (counters, so playtests can see how often a haptic was silently not played; Pillar 3). **The death cue is never dropped by a near-miss or a tap:** `HIT` has the highest priority. Source-verified facts: Android needs the export permission `VIBRATE` enabled or the call silently does nothing, an amplitude of -1 is the default level and otherwise it maps to 1-255; on Android a new vibration replaces a running one, so a preempting pulse truncates the pulse it preempts; iOS uses a CoreHaptics pulse that honors duration and amplitude and overlaps other pulses, except on old or unsupported hardware where it plays a fixed system vibration and ignores both. Haptics are an extra channel and never the only cue (Player Fantasy). **Ownership:** this GDD owns the mechanism, the `HapticsConfig` schema, the safety limits (`HAPTIC_MAX_MS`, `VIBRATE`) and the lifecycle gating. The per-kind values and priorities are provisional and move to Juice & Feedback's data when that GDD exists (Open Question 16).
7. **Display facts.** The core reads, through an injected `display_source` (a `Callable` returning a `Dictionary` with the keys `safe_area`, `screen_size`, `viewport_size`, `refresh_rate`; the node implements it with `DisplayServer.get_display_safe_area()`, a `Rect2i` in screen pixels, `screen_get_size()`, the viewport size and `screen_get_refresh_rate()`), at construction and once per event that has a regaining edge (`app_foregrounded` or `app_returned`, before those signals are emitted), and exposes them as read-only values (they can change: iOS forces the refresh rate to 60 in Low Power Mode, which can be toggled from Control Center without suspending the app). Camera and HUD read them at layout time; Platform Services does not interpret them. An empty safe area (`not has_area()`) is replaced by the full screen rectangle `Rect2i(Vector2i.ZERO, screen size)` in pixels; the viewport size stays in stretch units and is a separate value. Cutouts on iOS are UNVERIFIED.
8. **Project settings manifest.** `PlatformSettings` lists every setting the game requires as data (section, key, expected value, owner tag), and a CI lint checks it against `project.godot` and the export presets. Entries: `display/window/handheld/orientation` = 1 (portrait; value verified), `application/config/quit_on_go_back` = false, `application/run/max_fps` = 60, `input_devices/sensors/enable_gravity` = true (and `enable_accelerometer` if the sensor-source ADR keeps that fallback); in each Android export preset: `permissions/vibrate` = true, immersive mode on (`screen/immersive_mode` = true, the key name UNVERIFIED until the first preset exists) and Picture-in-Picture off (`supportsPictureInPicture` false in the custom Android build template; how 4.7.2 exposes it is UNVERIFIED). The lint extends Tilt Input AC-37d, which delegates to it for the entries tagged `tilt`. The `max_fps` knob range is 30-60, but the manifest holds one expected value: changing the knob means editing the manifest entry in the same commit. At boot the node reads the runtime-readable entries and passes them to `PlatformSettings.mismatches(manifest, read)`; it logs the result (Edge Cases) and never repairs a value except `quit_on_go_back` (rule 5).
9. **Frame cap.** `application/run/max_fps` is 60 for the MVP (technical preferences: 60 FPS; Tilt Input's noise filter is a 60 Hz claim). iOS already defaults to a preferred 60 fps (source-verified); whether a 60 cap holds on a 120 Hz Android device is UNVERIFIED (device check).
10. **Lifecycle hook for saving.** `app_backgrounded` is the only "flush now" signal Save & Persistence uses. Platform Services does no saving and does not wait for it.
11. **Testing seam.** `PlatformCore` takes `focus_implies_suspend`, a `clock` (a `Callable` returning integer microseconds; a real-time monotonic clock such as `Time.get_ticks_usec`, never simulation time, which can pause or scale), a `log_sink` (a `Callable(level, code, key, message)`), a `vibrate` (a `Callable(duration_ms, amplitude)`), the `display_source` of rule 7 and a validated `HapticsConfig`. GDScript has no typed `Callable` signatures, so the seams are checked by the tests; use method Callables, not lambdas that capture `self` in a `RefCounted` (reference cycle). Its events in are `on_focus_out()`, `on_focus_in()`, `on_paused()`, `on_resumed()` and `on_back_requested()`, plus `haptic(kind)` and `set_haptics_enabled(enabled)`; read-only getters `attentive`, `suspended`, `safe_area`, `screen_size`, `viewport_size`, `refresh_rate` and `haptic_drops(cause)`. The haptic gate is a pure function in `PlatformMath`. The node takes injected `keep_on` and `read_setting` Callables in tests. Tests build a fresh core each, from a named factory function (no `.tres` load).
12. **Keep the screen on.** A tilt-only run produces no touch events, so the OS dim and auto-lock timer would fire mid-run. At boot the node calls `DisplayServer.screen_set_keep_on(true)` once and never turns it off; the OS releases it when the app is suspended. The battery cost in Menu and Paused is accepted for the MVP (Open Question 19). A screen lock the player triggers is `FOCUS_OUT` and pauses the run, so only the dimming needs this rule.

### States and Transitions

The lifecycle state is derived from the two flags (`FIS` = `focus_implies_suspend`). **Under `FIS` true `paused` is always false, so only the first two rows are reachable on Android.**

| `focused` | `paused` | State | `attentive` | `suspended` (Android FIS true / iOS FIS false) |
|-----------|----------|-------|-------------|------|
| true | false | Active | true | false / false |
| false | false | Unfocused (iOS: Control Center, banner, call; Android: Home, lock, call, app switcher) | false | true / **false** |
| true | true | Regaining focus (iOS only: between `FOCUS_IN` and `RESUMED`) | false | (unreachable) / true |
| false | true | Backgrounded (iOS only) | false | (unreachable) / true |

Every event that changes neither `attentive` nor `suspended` emits nothing. Example, iOS Home then return: `FOCUS_OUT` gives `app_interrupted`; `PAUSED` gives `app_backgrounded`; `FOCUS_IN` gives nothing (still paused); `RESUMED` gives `app_foregrounded` then `app_returned`. Example, Android Home: `FOCUS_OUT` gives `app_interrupted` then `app_backgrounded`; a `PAUSED` or `RESUMED`, if the renderer sends one, is ignored; `FOCUS_IN` gives `app_foregrounded` then `app_returned`.

All interfaces are **provisional** (only Run State and Tilt Input have GDDs).

## Formulas

Only `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` is converted from seconds: once at load with `roundi(x * 1e6)`, then compared as integer microseconds (same rule as Tilt Input rule 8). The haptic durations are integer milliseconds passed straight through (`duration_ms` is an int; a fractional config value is rounded with `roundi` at load). Every default is a guess until a device test.

**F1. Lifecycle derivation and edge signals**

Events set the flags: `FOCUS_OUT` sets `focused = false`, `FOCUS_IN` sets `focused = true`; `PAUSED` sets `paused = true` and `RESUMED` sets `paused = false` **only when `FIS` is false** (ignored otherwise).

`attentive = focused and not paused`
`suspended = paused or (FIS and not focused)`

After each event, with `(a0, s0)` the values before and `(a1, s1)` after: emit `app_interrupted` iff `a0 and not a1`; `app_backgrounded` iff `not s0 and s1`; `app_foregrounded` iff `s0 and not s1`; `app_returned` iff `not a0 and a1`. Emission order: interrupted, backgrounded, foregrounded, returned (a single event never has both a losing and a regaining edge). `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` (debug) is logged, with the event name as its key, for an event whose flag already has the target value and for a `PAUSED`/`RESUMED` ignored under `FIS` true.

| Variable | Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|----------|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| Focus flag | `focused` | bool | {false, true} | true at construction; set by `FOCUS_IN`, cleared by `FOCUS_OUT` |
| Pause flag | `paused` | bool | {false, true} | false at construction; set by `PAUSED`, cleared by `RESUMED`; always false when `FIS` is true |
| Focus implies suspend | `FIS` | bool | {false, true} | fixed per platform: Android true, iOS false |
| Attentive | `attentive` | bool | | the app is in front and receiving input |
| Suspended | `suspended` | bool | | the OS has stopped sensors and rendering (iOS: only on a true background; Control Center leaves it false even though iOS stops rendering on `FOCUS_OUT`) |

**Output range:** 0, 1 or 2 signals per event (a losing pair or a regaining pair, never a mix); exactly 0 for a repeated event and for a `PAUSED/RESUMED` under `FIS` true.
**Example (iOS, FIS false):** `FOCUS_OUT` gives `app_interrupted` (attentive false, suspended false); `PAUSED` gives `app_backgrounded`; `FOCUS_IN` gives nothing (`paused` still true); `RESUMED` gives `app_foregrounded` then `app_returned`.
**Example (Android, FIS true):** `FOCUS_OUT` gives `app_interrupted` then `app_backgrounded`; a later `PAUSED` gives nothing (ignored); `FOCUS_IN` gives `app_foregrounded` then `app_returned`; a late `PAUSED`, `RESUMED` after that gives nothing (no phantom second interruption).
**Extremes:** `RESUMED` without a prior `PAUSED` and `FOCUS_IN` without a prior `FOCUS_OUT` are no-ops; `PAUSED` twice is a no-op. **Liveness:** from every reachable flag state, `FOCUS_IN` then `RESUMED` gives `attentive` true and `suspended` false.

**F2. Haptic gate**

```
gate_open = last_played_us is none
            or now_us < last_played_us
            or (now_us - last_played_us >= MIN_INTERVAL_us and now_us >= last_end_us)
play = enabled and attentive and dur_eff > 0 and (gate_open or prio > last_prio)
```

When `play`: `last_played_us = now_us`, `last_end_us = now_us + dur_eff * 1000`, `last_prio = prio`, and `Input.vibrate_handheld(dur_eff, amp_eff)` is called. A dropped call changes none of the three stamps.

| Variable | Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|----------|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| Haptics enabled | `enabled` | bool | | Settings switch, default true |
| Now | `now_us` | int (us) | >= 0 | injected real-time monotonic clock |
| Last pulse | `last_played_us` | int (us) or none | | stamp of the last pulse played |
| Last pulse end | `last_end_us` | int (us) | >= 0 | `last_played_us + dur_eff * 1000` of the last pulse played |
| Last priority | `last_prio` | int | 0 to 9 | priority of the last pulse played |
| Priority | `prio` | int | 0 to 9 | the calling kind's priority |
| Minimum interval | `MIN_INTERVAL_us` | int (us) | 0 to `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` max | `roundi(HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL * 1e6)` |
| Effective duration | `dur_eff` | int (ms) | 0 to `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` | F3 |

**Output range:** call or no call. Both comparisons are inclusive: exactly `MIN_INTERVAL_us` after the last pulse, and exactly at its end, plays. A strictly higher priority bypasses the gate (Android: it replaces and so truncates the running pulse; iOS: the pulses overlap); an equal or lower priority never does. The other drop causes (disabled, not attentive, zero duration) cannot be bypassed by priority.
**Example** (fixture: `MIN_INTERVAL` 0.05 s, NEAR_MISS 30 ms priority 1, HIT 80 ms priority 2): NEAR_MISS at 0 plays; HIT at 20000 us plays by priority (the gate is closed, 2 > 1); NEAR_MISS at 60000 is dropped (the HIT ends at 100000); NEAR_MISS at 100000 plays.
**Extremes:** if `now_us < last_played_us` (a clock anomaly; the clock is monotonic so this is defensive) the gate is open, the call plays and the three stamps are re-based to `now_us`. A `MIN_INTERVAL` of 0 never drops on the interval, but the pulse-end condition still applies: a same-priority call before the running pulse ends is dropped.

**F3. Effective duration and amplitude**

`dur_eff = clamp(duration_ms, 0, HAPTIC_MAX_MS)`; `amp_eff = -1` if `amplitude < 0`, `1.0` if `amplitude > 1`, else `amplitude`. A NaN or infinite config value is handled first, by config validation: it takes the kind's default with one `KNOB_CLAMPED` error per value (as in Tilt Input's log codes), so `effective()` never sees it. The engine maps `amp_eff` to the OS: Android level `clamp(int(amp_eff * 255), 1, 255)` (source-verified), `-1` is the OS default level; iOS passes an intensity clamped to 0-1 and on old hardware ignores it. `effective()` is a pure function of numbers: it is called with validated config values or direct numbers; `amp_eff` of -1 is reachable only when a caller passes a raw negative amplitude to it, never through `haptic(kind)`, and a loaded config is validated by the narrower ranges of Tuning Knobs (an amplitude of 0 is the faintest level, not silence, on Android).

**Output range:** `dur_eff` in `[0, HAPTIC_MAX_MS]`, `amp_eff` in `{-1}` and `[0, 1]`.
**Example** (`HAPTIC_MAX_MS` 200 for the example only): `duration_ms` 500 gives 200; `duration_ms` -5 gives 0 (dropped by F2); `amplitude` 0.5 gives Android level 127.

**F4. Effective frame rate (informational)**

`fps_eff = min(max_fps, refresh_rate)` when both are positive and finite, `max_fps` when the refresh rate is unknown (0, negative, NaN or infinite), `refresh_rate` when `max_fps` is 0 or less (the engine's "unlimited") and the refresh rate is known, and 0 (unknown, no frame time) when neither is known; frame time is `1 / fps_eff` when `fps_eff > 0`. With `max_fps` 60: a 60 Hz screen gives 16.667 ms, a 120 Hz screen gives 16.667 ms if the cap holds (UNVERIFIED on Android), a 30 Hz screen gives 33.333 ms. On a 90, 75 or 144 Hz panel pacing quantizes and the value is only an estimate. Consumers (Tilt Input's noise rejection, Run State's `DT_MAX` clamp) work from the injected clock, not from this value; it only tells a tester what to expect.

## Edge Cases

**Lifecycle**
- **If `FOCUS_OUT` arrives twice (iOS `willResignActive` then `didEnterBackground`)**: the second changes nothing and emits nothing (one `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` debug log), so one `app_interrupted`.
- **If `PAUSED` arrives while already unfocused (iOS true background: `FOCUS_OUT` then `PAUSED`)**: only `app_backgrounded` is emitted; `app_interrupted` was already sent.
- **If `RESUMED` arrives without a prior `PAUSED`, `FOCUS_IN` without a prior `FOCUS_OUT`, or `PAUSED` twice**: a no-op with one `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` debug log; nothing is emitted (F1).
- **If iOS returns in the order `FOCUS_IN` then `RESUMED`**: `FOCUS_IN` emits nothing (still paused); `RESUMED` emits `app_foregrounded` then `app_returned` together. Consumers must not assume the two arrive in separate frames.
- **If iOS returns in the order `RESUMED` then `FOCUS_IN` (probable: UIKit calls `willEnterForeground` before `didBecomeActive`)**: `RESUMED` emits `app_foregrounded` while the app is still not `attentive`; `FOCUS_IN` then emits `app_returned`. `app_foregrounded` therefore does not mean attentive, and Tilt Input's settle must count only while `attentive` (Open Question 24).
- **If a banner or Control Center on iOS takes focus for a moment**: `app_interrupted` then, when it closes, `app_returned`; no `app_backgrounded` (`FIS` false). Run State pauses on the first and stays Paused; `app_returned` only re-anchors its Paused guard.
- **If the Android notification shade is pulled down, or a heads-up call arrives**: expected to emit nothing (UNVERIFIED: no `onWindowFocusChanged` handling was found in the source, and `WM_WINDOW_FOCUS_*` is a desktop and web notification), so the run may continue under the shade with tilt still live. Sticky immersive mode (Core Rules 8) makes the first top-edge swipe show only transient bars; PS-1 measures whether the shade can still reach the play area. If a device does emit `FOCUS_OUT`, it is treated as an interruption.
- **If Android sends `PAUSED/RESUMED` (GL renderer) or never sends them (Vulkan)**: both are handled: with `FIS` true they are ignored and the model uses `FOCUS_OUT/IN` alone (F1).
- **If a late `PAUSED, RESUMED` pair arrives on Android after `FOCUS_IN` (another thread)**: ignored; no second interruption, Save flush or Tilt buffer clear.
- **If `FOCUS_IN` arrives but `RESUMED` never does (iOS only; an OS violation, iOS pairs them)**: the app stays not `attentive` and `suspended`; Tilt Input never leaves Acquiring, and its timeout and Menu path are the recovery (Tilt Input rule 10 and edge cases). Not handled here.
- **If a lifecycle event arrives before the composition root has connected the receivers (boot)**: the core still tracks the flags; a signal with no listener is lost and never replayed. Every receiver reads `attentive` and `suspended` when it connects.
- **If the app is launched while unfocused**: the core starts with `focused` true; the flags are corrected by the first event (the engine sends `FOCUS_OUT/IN` on change). Seeding from the window state at boot is left to the composition-root story (Open Question 21).
- **If the OS terminates the app while it is suspended**: no code runs and no signal is sent (Run State Open Question 6).
- **If Android puts the app in Picture-in-Picture**: Picture-in-Picture is disabled in the export manifest (Core Rules 8), so it should not happen; PS-5 verifies it. If a device enters it anyway and emits `FOCUS_OUT`, it is an interruption.
- **If Android runs the app in split-screen (Android 10+ multi-resume)**: an unfocused split-screen app stays RESUMED and emits no `FOCUS_OUT`, so the game would keep running while the player uses the other window. Not supported for the MVP; PS-1 checks it and the export preset's resizability is reviewed (Open Question 23).
- **If the OS ignores `screen_set_keep_on` (battery saver, device policy) and dims the screen**: a run continues under a dim screen; a lock is `FOCUS_OUT` and pauses. PS-1 measures it with a device screen timeout of 30 s.

**Back**
- **If Back arrives twice in a frame**: two `back_pressed` signals; consumers are idempotent (Run State treats the second in Paused as a silent no-op).
- **If `application/config/quit_on_go_back` is left true**: the engine quits before the game sees Back; the settings lint fails the build (Acceptance Criteria), the node sets it false at boot and the boot check logs `SETTINGS_MISMATCH`.
- **If Back arrives in Menu, Hit, Paused or Boot and Menus & Screen Flow has not connected**: nothing happens (with `quit_on_go_back` false the engine ignores it), so the app cannot be left by Back. Menus must handle it (Run State Open Question 16; a proposed default is in Open Question 8).
- **If the Android 13+ predictive back gesture, or Android 16 with target SDK 36, does not deliver `WM_GO_BACK_REQUEST`**: Core Rules 5; the run pauses as for Home but `back_pressed` is unavailable; device check PS-4.

**Haptics**
- **If `haptic(kind)` is called while the app is not `attentive`**: dropped, never queued, no log; counted as `NOT_ATTENTIVE`.
- **If `kind` is not in `HapticsConfig`**: dropped with one `UNKNOWN_HAPTIC_KIND` error (rate limited per kind id) and counted as `UNKNOWN_KIND`.
- **If two haptic calls arrive close together**: the second is dropped when its priority is not strictly higher than the last played pulse's and the gate (interval and pulse end) is closed (F2). A `HIT` after a `NEAR_MISS` always plays; a `NEAR_MISS` or `UI_TAP` after a `HIT` within the interval or the pulse is dropped. Juice & Feedback does not need to sequence calls; it owns the priority values.
- **If haptics are disabled while a pulse is running**: the pulse finishes; nothing cancels it, and no extra `vibrate` call is made.
- **If the Android export permission `VIBRATE` is unchecked**: the OS call silently does nothing and nothing detects it at runtime; the settings lint on the export preset is the guard.
- **If the device has no vibration hardware, or an old iPhone plays the fixed system vibration**: accepted; the old-iOS pulse ignores duration and amplitude (device matrix, spike).
- **If a config value is NaN or infinite**: it takes the kind's default with one `KNOB_CLAMPED` error (F3).

**Display facts and settings**
- **If `get_display_safe_area()` returns an empty rectangle (no cutout information, desktop, or a platform without an override)**: the safe area is the full screen rectangle in pixels (rule 7).
- **If `screen_get_refresh_rate()` returns 0, a negative value or a non-finite value (unknown)**: `fps_eff` is `max_fps` (F4).
- **If the refresh rate changes without the app suspending (iOS Low Power Mode toggled from Control Center)**: the values are re-read on `app_returned` as well as on `app_foregrounded` (rule 7), before the signals are emitted.
- **If a required project setting differs at runtime (orientation not portrait, `max_fps` not 60, `quit_on_go_back` true, a sensor setting off)**: the node logs one `SETTINGS_MISMATCH` error per key at boot, from `PlatformSettings.mismatches`. It is a diagnostic; the lint is the gate. Tilt Input's own `NOT_PORTRAIT` remains as it is.
- **If two systems both handle a lifecycle notification (a lint miss)**: duplicate signals from the second owner; all receivers are idempotent on the four signals.
- **If a receiver of `app_backgrounded` (Save & Persistence) is slow**: Platform Services does not wait; how much time the OS grants before suspending is not measured (Open Question); Save & Persistence must keep its flush short.

## Tuning Knobs

All defaults are guesses until a device test; none comes from a keyboard or an emulator. Every knob lives in `HapticsConfig` (a `Resource`) or in the project settings manifest, never in code. Validation at load: each value is clamped to its safe range; a NaN or infinite value takes its default; one `KNOB_CLAMPED` error per value changed, validated in this order: `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` first, then the per-kind values.

| Knob | Default | Safe range | Affects | Too low | Too high |
|------|---------|------------|---------|---------|----------|
| `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` | 0.08 s | 0-0.5 | Minimum time between two same-priority pulses (F2) | A burst of events buzzes continuously | A distinct near-miss is dropped after a hit or a tap |
| `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` | 200 ms | 50-500 | Cap on any pulse duration (F3) | Long effects are cut | A config error produces a long vibration that drains the battery |
| `NEAR_MISS` duration / amplitude / priority | 30 ms / 0.5 / 1 | 10-`HAPTIC_MAX_MS` / 0-1 / 0-9 | The near-miss pulse (provisional; Juice & Feedback owns the feel) | Not felt; some Android devices ignore very short effects | Reads as a hit, tiring on frequent near-misses |
| `HIT` duration / amplitude / priority | 80 ms / 1.0 / 2 | 10-`HAPTIC_MAX_MS` / 0-1 / 0-9 | The death pulse | Weak sting; a priority not above `NEAR_MISS` lets the death cue be dropped | Unpleasant; must stay short so restart is not felt as delayed |
| `UI_TAP` duration / amplitude / priority | 15 ms / 0.3 / 0 | 0-`HAPTIC_MAX_MS` / 0-1 / 0-9 | Menu taps (0 disables) | Not felt | Annoying on every tap |
| `haptics_enabled` | true | true / false | Master switch, supplied by Settings & Accessibility | | |
| `max_fps` (project setting) | 60 | 30-60 | The frame cap (Core Rules 9, F4); the manifest holds one expected value (Core Rules 8) | Lower Tilt Input noise rejection (30 Hz passes twice the alternating noise of 60 Hz, Tilt Input F3) and more input lag | Heat and battery on 120 Hz devices |

**Fixed constants (not tuning knobs):** the log rate limit of 1.0 s per code and detail key (the key is the kind id for `UNKNOWN_HAPTIC_KIND`, `<kind>.<field>` for `KNOB_CLAMPED`, the setting key for `SETTINGS_MISMATCH` and the event name for `LIFECYCLE_NOOP`; the window runs from the last message that passed for that key), with the log codes `UNKNOWN_HAPTIC_KIND`, `KNOB_CLAMPED`, `SETTINGS_MISMATCH` and `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` (debug level); `focus_implies_suspend` per platform (Android true, iOS false); the haptic drop causes; the required project settings of the manifest (orientation 1, `quit_on_go_back` false, sensor settings, `VIBRATE` permission, immersive mode, Picture-in-Picture off): these are correctness requirements checked by the lint, not values to tune.

**Knob interactions**
- `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` and the pulse length decide which of two same-priority events is heard: the first one wins. A strictly higher priority (a `HIT`) always plays, so Juice & Feedback does not need to order its calls (F2).
- `max_fps` interacts with Tilt Input (its filter is a 60 Hz claim; its lowest supported rate `F_MIN` is 20 Hz) and with Run State's `DT_MAX` (0.1 s): any value from 30 to 60 stays inside both, a value below 20 would not.
- A per-kind duration above `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` is clamped, so `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` bounds every kind.
- Old iPhones and devices without a haptic engine ignore amplitude and duration (fixed pulse), so per-kind tuning only matters on capable devices.

**Sources of truth elsewhere:** `haptics_enabled` (Settings & Accessibility), `DT_MAX` and `RESUME_COUNTDOWN` (Run State), `F_MIN` (Tilt Input), the final feel, values and priorities of haptic events (Juice & Feedback, when authored; the values above are provisional).

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**Visual:** none. Platform Services has no visuals; what the player sees of a lifecycle event (the pause screen) belongs to Run State and HUD. **Audio:** no audio of its own. The engine already stops or pauses audio when the app loses focus (source-verified: iOS stops rendering and audio on `FOCUS_OUT`, Android pauses audio on `onPause` unless in Picture-in-Picture), so this system adds no audio handling. The OS audio policy (the player's own music, the iOS silent switch, headphone or Bluetooth disconnect mid-run, Android audio focus, the iOS audio session category) has no owner yet (Open Question 20). **Haptics** are the one output channel it owns: three provisional kinds (`NEAR_MISS`, `HIT`, `UI_TAP`) with values in `HapticsConfig`; Juice & Feedback owns when they fire and how they feel. Haptics have no assets, so no `/asset-spec` is needed.

## UI Requirements

No player-facing UI of its own. Requests to other systems:

1. **Settings & Accessibility:** a haptics on/off switch that feeds `haptics_enabled` (default on); a reduced-haptics intensity is Open Question 16.
2. **Menus & Screen Flow:** handle `back_pressed` in every phase except Running and Resuming (Menu, Paused, Hit, Boot) and own the quit decision, using `quit()` (Run State Open Question 16); the app cannot be left by Back otherwise.
3. **HUD and Camera:** keep interactive and important elements inside the safe area (notch, rounded corners, the gesture bar), keep touch targets (the pause button) away from the system gesture edges, and read the safe area after each `app_foregrounded` / `app_returned`.

No UX Flag: there is no screen or HUD element of its own to specify.

## Acceptance Criteria

**Targets:** **[M]** `PlatformMath` static functions and `PlatformSettings.mismatches`; **[C]** `PlatformCore` with injected `fis`, `clock` (us), `log_sink` (a recording sink without the rate limiter), `vibrate` and `display_source`; **[R]** `RateLimitedLog` with an injected clock and sink; **[N]** the `PlatformServices` node with a spy core and injected `keep_on` and `read_setting`; **[I]** integration; **[L]** CI lint in `tools/ci/`. Tests live in `tests/unit/platform_services/` and `tests/integration/platform_services/`, named `platform_services_[feature]_test.gd`. **Fixture** (a named factory function, no `.tres` load): `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` 0.05 s, `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` 200, NEAR_MISS 30/0.5/priority 1, HIT 80/1.0/priority 2, UI_TAP 15/0.3/priority 0, screen 1080x1920 and viewport 540x960 (different, so a swapped value fails). Events FO, FI, P, R = FOCUS_OUT, FOCUS_IN, PAUSED, RESUMED; signals INT, BG, FG, RET = `app_interrupted`, `app_backgrounded`, `app_foregrounded`, `app_returned`; BACK = `back_pressed`. Integer microsecond stamps; exact for integers, lists and the outputs of `effective`, 1e-6 for other floats; a fresh core per case.

**Lifecycle (F1, R3, R4)**
- **AC-1 [C]** signals emitted per event (`-` = none, `|` separates events); rows are named for the device checks. iOS (`fis` false): **I1** `FO,P,FI,R` gives INT | BG | - | FG,RET; **I2** `FO,P,R,FI` gives INT | BG | FG | RET; **I3** `FO,FI` gives INT | RET; **I4** `FO,FO,P,FI,R` gives INT | - | BG | - | FG,RET; **I5** `P,P` gives INT,BG | -. Android (`fis` true; P and R are ignored): **A1** `FO,FI` gives INT,BG | FG,RET; **A2** `FO,P,FI,R` gives INT,BG | - | FG,RET | -; **A3** `P,FO,R,FI` gives - | INT,BG | - | FG,RET; **A4** `FO,P,FI` gives INT,BG | - | FG,RET; **A5** `FO,FI,P,R` gives INT,BG | FG,RET | - | - (no second interruption). Both: `R` or `FI` on a fresh core emits nothing and logs one `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` (key = the event name); so do `FO,FO`, `P,P` and, on Android, every `P` and `R`; construction emits nothing (`attentive` true, `suspended` false); each signal is recorded before the `on_*` call returns.
- **AC-2 [C]** exhaustive, both `fis`: every sequence of 1-6 events (4+16+64+256+1024+4096 = 5460), in a fixed enumeration order that prints `fis` and the sequence on a failure: at most 2 signals per event, never a losing and a regaining signal together, fixed order INT, BG, FG, RET; the running count of INT minus RET and of BG minus FG stays in {0, 1} and equals 1 iff not `attentive` (respectively iff `suspended`); with `fis` true, INT and BG (and FG and RET) always share an event, and P and R never change any getter; liveness: from every reachable flag state, FI then R gives `attentive` true and `suspended` false. Plus an independent transition table (state x event x `fis`, literal expected flags and signals, not F1 restated), so a wrong flag table cannot pass the invariants.

**Haptics (F2, F3, R6)**
- **AC-3 [M]** `haptic_gate(enabled, attentive, dur, prio, now, last, last_end, last_prio, min_us)` with `min_us` 50000: last none, now 0 plays; last 0 (last_end 30000, last_prio 1), prio 1: now 49999 drops, 50000 and 50001 play; last 0 (last_end 80000, last_prio 2), prio 1: now 60000 drops (past the interval, inside the pulse), now 80000 plays; last 0 (last_end 30000, last_prio 1), now 20000: prio 2 plays, prio 1 drops, prio 0 drops; last 100000 and now 99999 plays (clock anomaly); `min_us` 0, last 100000, last_end 130000: now 129999 drops and now 130000 plays; `enabled` false, `attentive` false, `dur` 0 and `dur` -1 each drop even with prio 9. `interval_us(0.05, 0.08, 0.5, 0)` = 50000, 80000, 500000, 0.
- **AC-4 [C]** HIT at stamp 0 plays (`vibrate` receives 80, 1.0); NEAR_MISS at 30000 drops (`THROTTLED`, and `last_played_us` is unchanged), at 79999 drops (past the interval, inside the HIT's pulse) and at 80000 plays. On a fresh core: NEAR_MISS at 0 plays, then HIT at 20000 plays (priority 2 over 1; `vibrate` receives 80, 1.0), then HIT at 40000 drops (equal priority, inside the interval) and NEAR_MISS at 60000 drops (the HIT of 20000 ends at 100000). NEAR_MISS at 0, NEAR_MISS at 40000 drops and at 50000 plays; UI_TAP within a NEAR_MISS's interval drops. After each other drop cause (disabled, not attentive, duration 0 through a `UI_TAP` set to 0, unknown kind) an allowed call at the same stamp on the same fresh core plays; the drop order is `UNKNOWN_KIND`, `DISABLED`, `NOT_ATTENTIVE`, `ZERO_DURATION`, `THROTTLED`, each incrementing exactly its `haptic_drops` counter; disabled, not-attentive and zero-duration drops log nothing; an unknown kind logs one `UNKNOWN_HAPTIC_KIND` error and does not call `vibrate`. Each drop cause has its own test.
- **AC-5 [M]** `effective(dur, amp, max)`: `dur` 500, 201, 200, 1, 0, -5 gives 200, 200, 200, 1, 0, 0; `amp` 0.5, 0, 1, 1.0000001, 2, -0.0001, -1 gives 0.5, 0, 1, 1, 1, -1, -1 (exact equality, so a clamped 1 is told from 1.0000001); `max` 50 with `dur` 80 gives 50. NaN and infinite config values are covered by AC-7.
- **AC-6 [R]** log codes are exactly `UNKNOWN_HAPTIC_KIND`, `KNOB_CLAMPED`, `SETTINGS_MISMATCH` (error level) and `LIFECYCLE_NOOP` (debug). `RateLimitedLog` passes one message per (code, key) per 1.0 s of the injected clock, the window running from the last message that passed: a message at 0 passes; the same key at 999999 us does not; at 1000000 us it does; two different keys (or codes) at one stamp both pass (for example unknown kind 99 twice within 1 s gives one log and kind 98 gives a second); a clock that goes backwards passes the message and re-bases the window; the level is preserved. AC-1, AC-4 and AC-7 use a recording sink without the limiter.
- **AC-7 [C]** (config validation, on `HapticsConfig`) one row per knob, an out-of-range value gives one `KNOB_CLAMPED` and the boundary gives none: `HAPTIC_MIN_INTERVAL` -0.01 and 0.51 become 0 and 0.5; `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` 49 and 501 become 50 and 500; NEAR_MISS and HIT duration 9 and 201 become 10 and 200; UI_TAP duration -1 becomes 0 (0 is valid) and 201 becomes 200; an amplitude of 1.1 becomes 1 and -0.5 becomes 0; a priority of -1 and 10 become 0 and 9; a NaN or infinite value takes its default (the error key is `<kind>.<field>`). `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` is validated first: with `HAPTIC_MAX_MS` 50 and HIT 80, HIT becomes 50, one error, and `vibrate` receives 50.
- The Android amplitude-to-level mapping (F3) is engine behavior and is not an AC; PS-6 observes it.
